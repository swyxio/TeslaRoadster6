# Bay geography and contributor contract

The map is a compressed arcade interpretation, not navigation data or a 1:1 recreation. Relative relationships follow the [Golden Gate Bridge District](https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/bridge-operations/traffic-management/) and [Metropolitan Transportation Commission](https://www.mtc.ca.gov/operations/programs-projects/bridges/san-francisco-oakland-bay-bridge): Golden Gate links San Francisco and Marin across the strait; the Bay Bridge links San Francisco and Oakland via Yerba Buena Island, with a western suspension section and an eastern span. Local streets, coastlines, widths, elevation and landmark positions are artistic approximations.

## Bay circuit and source

`HIGHWAYS` traces northbound [US 101 relation 108619](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/108619) and [I-880 relation 74660](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/74660), joined by fictional city connections at SF, San Jose and Oakland. The source extract in `shared/bay-route-source.json` records relation, way and node versions and coordinates retrieved 2026-10-17. The I-880 relation had one approximately 66.9 m gap between nodes 12433107327 and 1688870550; the game joins those endpoints with a straight interpolation. Regenerate `shared/bay-highway-traces.ts` with `python3 shared/bake-bay-highways.py`.

The bake projects coordinates approximately to meters, then independently rotates and scales each corridor to fit the existing game-city anchors (roughly 1:6.8–1:6.9) and initially simplifies it with a 35 m game-space tolerance. It removes one sharp Oakland kink, rounds inland bends, and replaces the abrupt San Jose meeting point with a broad fictional connection; these artistic changes can move the final road beyond that simplification tolerance. Both traffic directions share a fictional 32 m-wide surface; lane counts, destination labels, district skylines and shorelines are authored for play. They do not represent exact locations, ramps or carriageways. The closed SF → Peninsula → San Jose → East Bay → Oakland → Bay Bridge route measures about **19.86 game km** along `BAY_CIRCUIT`, including the city connections. The two city street turns are right angles, so slow down there. In the earlier protocol-v5 Bay build, a controlled test starting the San Jose connection at 160 or 220 km/h stayed within the road in both directions; an approach at full 259 km/h boost left the pavement. Those observations do not verify the merged Rocket physics. Other Bay crossings and real grade-separated interchanges are not implemented.

Hand-shaped I-280 and El Camino Real add two connected Peninsula alternatives to US 101; their access opens in stages with the surrounding areas. They are compressed fictional alignments rather than the real interchanges or surveyed centerlines; [Caltrans describes the US 101 Bayshore Freeway](https://dot.ca.gov/caltrans-near-me/district-4/d4-news/2026-08-19-us101-nb-bayshore-freeway-hospital-curve-upcoming-full-closures) and [State Route 82 / El Camino Real and nearby I-280](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/district-4/documents/d4-environmental-docs/82-el-camino-real-roadway-renewal-project/2021-06-09-0k810_1g900_sm-82_ded_061021_ada.pdf). Two fictional local streets connect the routes near the middle of the Peninsula. Players can take either split manually or pin a destination on the route; self-driving does not offer route preferences or congestion-based choices. The existing bounded ambient fleet is distributed across all three corridors.

The [Caltrain station names](https://www.caltrain.com/stops) identify eight compressed game-space stops from San Francisco 4th & King through South San Francisco, Burlingame, Redwood City, Palo Alto, Mountain View and Santa Clara to San José Diridon. `shared/caltrain.ts` owns their platforms, local roads, seven playable parking lots and a decorative rail line. The ordinary parking games and rocket pickups work at those lots; both highway directions can navigate their approaches. The Redwood City station has a stylized clock tower inspired by a [Caltrain planning photograph](https://www.caltrain.com/files/caltrain/Assets/5%2B-%2BRedwood%2BCity%2BPlanning%2Band%2BReal%2BEstate%2BUpdate%2BPowerPoint.pdf), decorative redwoods, and a compressed downtown cluster with Fox Theatre and the County History Museum. Trees clear roads, lots, platforms, buildings and rails; they do not collide. Local roads, town buildings, platforms and rail positions are fictional, and there is no train or timetable simulation. The San Francisco stop is available at level 0, South San Francisco at 2, Burlingame and Redwood City at 3, Palo Alto at 4, Mountain View and Santa Clara at 5, and Diridon at 6. Short optional northbound and southbound sky lanes sit above the stops, with the same player steering and locked-region safeguards as other currents; they are flight routes, not rail service.

The first-party code and hand-authored map content remain covered by the repository's MIT license. The SF Kart data pipeline informed the approach; no SF Kart assets are included.

`shared/map.ts` is the single authority for bounds, land polygons, road segments, bridge decks, parks and collision buildings. Positive X is east and positive Z south. `isSurface(x,z)` defines ground coverage; `supportHeight` and `supportNormal` describe its height and slope as well as the crossing, stunt ramp, island bank, island oval, island jumps, dome exterior and building roofs. `supportId` distinguishes these surfaces, the stunt loop, island cliff, dome interior, ferry and airborne entities. Loop and cliff height and orientation depend on `loopAngle`, and dome interior support on `domeS`, since those surfaces can have more than one height at one x/z; generic x/z height and normal lookups return null for them and the moving ferry. Most land and both Bay crossings remain flat. Alongside the original hill south of SF, three drivable hills evoke Twin Peaks, Mount Tamalpais and Mission Peak; their shared terrain triangles define rendered ground and physical support. A fictional overpass crosses US 101. These features are arcade-scale interpretations, not real elevation data. The overpass approaches connect to the surrounding ground, not to an interchange. The visible water surface is Y=−4. Grounded vehicles stop at unsupported shores; an airborne entity landing in open water returns to a free spawn with air and condition preserved. `nearbyBuildings` is the shared spatial collision index. The authoritative room simulation and renderer import the same data; clients never decide a landing or a hit.

`src/bay-world.ts` builds the landscape from those definitions, with instanced roads, buildings and windows. `src/main.ts` builds its minimap from the same polygons and roads. `shared/traffic.ts` consumes the shared Bay route in addition to city loops; `shared/navigation.ts` derives a directed lane graph for destinations, respecting region unlocks and disconnected roads. Parked vehicles are collision-checked on creation; the first available spawn faces into San Francisco toward the initial delivery. Twenty charger identities are sourced from Tesla, and four air stations in Berkeley, Mill Valley and Corte Madera are explicitly fictional. The 24 shared lot footprints provide marked parking activities and Rocket pickups. Selected sites have stylized venue buildings whose collision and rendered footprints are shared. Placement, surroundings, four game bays per lot and air refills are fictional; see [station sources](STATIONS.md).

The distant horizon is an original generated illustration wrapped around a fixed, elongated enclosure outside the playable map: Marin to the north, the East Bay hills to the east, the South Bay to the south, and the Peninsula ranges to the west. It shifts in bearing as the camera moves or flies and follows the game clock's day/night lighting. It is decorative, not surveyed terrain, a collision surface, a true 360-degree photograph or an exact depiction of individual peaks. `src/horizon-geometry.ts` projects the enclosure inside the existing camera range while keeping its world-space bearings; the fog, camera range, shared terrain, roads and water do not change. The repeating image and compressed map make the view an approximation rather than geographic navigation. Asset provenance is in [the manifest](../public/assets/manifest.md).

**The Vault · Palo Alto.** The candidate branch replaces one generated midrise with the fictional Vault showroom. `LUXURY_SHOWROOM` in `shared/luxury.ts` defines the 24 × 22 × 9 m building centered at `x=-828.56, z=4003.44` and a 10 m radius gold forecourt centered at `x=-829, z=4030`. `shared/map.ts` inserts that building into the authoritative collision index; `src/bay-world.ts` renders the building and ring from the same values. The forecourt remains open ground. The location and dimensions are invented for the compressed map.

The intended purchase flow requires a stopped player at the forecourt. After purchase, owners can request their vehicles at the Vault or at a physical workshop; remote workshop access does not grant remote collection. The wallet and transaction flow remain in progress. Verify approaches, surrounding Caltrain and workshop geometry, the marker, mobile interaction and collision in the game; visual and performance verification are pending. See [LUXURY_SHOWROOM.md](LUXURY_SHOWROOM.md).

North Bay Playground is a fictional, oversized playground north of San Francisco. Its shared polygon spans about 1,400 × 1,120 game meters, including the island ferry apron; `isRocketIsland` covers that polygon for safety checks at any altitude. Its loop and dock road use shared road geometry. `FERRY_DOCKS` records both apron tips and the centers for a 38 m deck. The SF apron joins the x=0 street; the island apron joins the loop. Water separates the two and the island remains separate from Marin and the Golden Gate. `MAP_BOUNDS.minZ` is −2,400 for the island. These positions are game fiction, not real island or ferry geography.

The island has two authored stunt surfaces in `shared/island-stunts.ts`; `src/island-stunts.ts` renders their exact shared vertices. The east–west bank runs from x=−20 to 180 at z=−1,750. Its 28 m lane rises across its width, tapers to ground at both ends, and has sloped shoulders. Ground driving and swept landings follow its triangulated height and normal; jumping uses that normal. The 24 m-wide cliff ribbon starts at (420, −1,850), curves upward with a 20 m radius, and extends to a 225° overhang. Cars enter eastbound from its foot; an ordinary car detaches at the vertical, while an active Rocket can follow the overhang. Jumping or losing Rocket capability releases the car with its world velocity. These are bounded arcade surfaces, not a general terrain or adhesion solver.

The speed oval uses shared triangles from `shared/oval-bank.ts` for physical support, normals and swept landings; `src/island-stunts.ts` renders the same lattice with a small vertical offset to avoid flicker. Its 28 m lane banks outward, with shoulders tapering to ground. The start/grid, short-course overlap and dock junction stay flat, blending into the bank over 70 m. The existing oval centerline remains the race and minimap route. Steering and traction remain arcade behavior rather than a realistic banking model.

`shared/island-jumps.ts` defines two 40 m eastbound launch ramps on the island's infield. Each rises to a 4 m lip, and the renderer and orange minimap footprints use the same coordinates. Both normal and Rocket cars can drive up, leave with the slope's upward momentum and land; swept top contact and normals use the same shared surface. Swept triangular sides and high ends block vehicles in the air or on the ground, including cars pushed by another vehicle. Their approaches and landing corridors avoid the banked oval and its shoulders, dock, cliff and parking lots.

The landmarks add stylized, collision-backed versions of [UCSF Parnassus](https://www.ucsf.edu/about/locations), [Stanford's Hoover Tower](https://community.stanford.edu/opportunities/hoover-tower), [UC Berkeley's Campanile](https://visit.berkeley.edu/campus-attractions/campanile), [SFO's control tower](https://www.flysfo.com/about-sfo/awards/new-control-tower-sfo-honored-top-engineering-project-america), [Fremont's Mission San José](https://www.fremont.gov/government/departments/economic-development/real-estate-development-investment/historic-districts/better-block) and the [San José City Hall rotunda](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/event-services/facilities-and-fees/rotunda). `LANDMARKS` in `shared/map.ts` owns their game-space footprints and clear arrival points. Their dimensions and placements are invented, and SFO's runway is a driving area with no aviation simulation. Lake Merritt's miniature islands are inspired by its [bird islands](https://www.oaklandca.gov/Community/Parks-Facilities/Parks/Lakeside-Park); the lake and islands are decorative over drivable ground. Larry’s Imaginary Yacht is a fictional Easter egg, not a depiction of Larry Ellison's real yacht or its location.

Curved roofs for the arena, San Jose rotunda, Coit and Salesforce caps, and selected northern domed landmarks follow their shared arcade surface approximations. Other landmark collisions retain simple building envelopes, so they are not a general mesh solver.

The 100 additional places comprise 50 northern and 50 Peninsula, Fremont and South Bay identities in `shared/north-landmarks.ts` and `shared/south-landmarks.ts`. Of these, 99 have shared building collision envelopes; Mission Peak uses terrain instead. Twin Peaks and Mount Tamalpais add two named summit markers. They supplement the pre-existing landmarks above. Original low-poly models use recognizable silhouettes and nameplates, but the redistributed sites are not surveyed or exact-sized. Their sources are indexed in [northern references](BAY_LANDMARKS_NORTH.md) and [southern references](BAY_LANDMARKS_SOUTH.md).

A further 100 original, compact landmark interpretations are indexed with sources in [additional Bay landmarks](BAY_LANDMARKS_ADDITIONAL.md). Their shared positions drive the world models and zoomed-in map labels. Eighty-nine solid sites use 337 explicitly authored structural parts, at most eight per site, shared by instanced rendering and physics. Decks, fields, walls and curved roofs provide bounded volumes and support; raised canopies leave open air beneath them. Fine trim, markings, miniature vehicles and eleven open-air gardens, groves, rides and skeletal monuments are decorative and do not collide. Vehicle contact uses horizontal footprints and sampled surface heights rather than exact mesh intersections; airborne tilt uses vertical bounds. These compact interpretations are arcade approximations, not exact architectural surveys. The existing coastline has no separate Alameda or Richmond landmass and only a small Marin headland, so those sites and outlying locations such as Pigeon Point and Lick Observatory are relocated onto supported areas of the compressed game map. Locations and listed access are not navigation or visitor guidance.

Five original miniature campuses evoke Stripe in South San Francisco, Meta/Facebook in Menlo Park, Google in Mountain View, NVIDIA in Santa Clara and Apple Park in Cupertino. Their fictional locations and building envelopes are shared with collision, the renderer and map; Apple Park's ring has a ground-level entrance. See [campus references](TECH_CAMPUSES.md). These are not surveyed sites or affiliated venues.

Four further compact, collision-backed models evoke [Chabot Space & Science Center](https://chabotspace.org/visit/observatories/) and the [Oakland Zoo](https://www.oaklandzoo.org/explore-the-zoo/) in the East Bay, [NASA Ames](https://www.nasa.gov/ames/) beside the existing Moffett Hangar One, and [Levi's Stadium](https://levisstadium.com/contact-us/) near Santa Clara. The zoo's gondola and the Ames wind tunnel are visual details, not operating rides or physics volumes. Placement and architecture are original arcade approximations.

`shared/billboards.ts` places decorative, non-colliding signs on an SF street and beside US 101, I-880, I-280 and El Camino Real, using 12 original designs for entirely fictional parody companies. The placement checks supported ground and clears road surfaces, buildings and gameplay markers; their posts are decorative. They are not real advertisements or sponsors.

In the fictional 2050 setting, `TESLAND_MONUMENTS` places the First Delivery Monument on the Marin headlands, clear of the Golden Gate approach. Its rectangular base uses shared building collision and roof support, and the minimap uses the same position. The car and plaque above it are decorative and do not collide. This is original in-game satire, not a real landmark or affiliated installation.

The existing Marin headlands have fictional local streets connected to the Golden Gate approach, with separate Mill Valley and Corte Madera arrival areas in `shared/marin-roads.ts`. Their flat, compressed layouts are authored for driving and are not surveyed geography. The separate North Bay Playground island remains a playground rather than a delivery town.

Eleven additional town street loops, a short airport-side loop and landmark access streets in `shared/town-delivery-roads.ts`, `shared/sfo-delivery-road.ts` and `shared/landmark-access-roads.ts` connect local arrivals to the Bay roads. An additional Berkeley route uses existing street pavement. Their block layouts and business placements are fictional, compressed game geography. Where these streets cross the decorative Caltrain line, there is no train, gate or signaling simulation. Airport pickups use roadside handoffs; cars do not enter the terminals.

The Marin headlands and fictional playground island have low-poly redwood and oak groves; the island also has stylized vineyard rows inspired by the North Bay. This is invented scenery, not an accurate depiction of Napa or Alcatraz. The instanced trees and vines stand on shared supported ground and clear roads, race gates, parking lots and stunt approaches. They are decorative, like the existing park trees, and do not create invisible or mismatched physical obstacles.

`FREMONT_SPACEPORT` defines a fictional factory, launch pad, tower and clear boarding ring near the compressed Fremont district. Factory, pad and tower are shared building envelopes; the rocket, construction gantry, animated workers and vehicles, tanks, tower arms and signs are decorative. The visible hull and the tower's vertical dollar scale follow the last confirmed game-wide earnings total; the separate room donation fund does not alter them. An outline and gantry show the project even at very low progress, and the sign beside the ring explains the goal. Both maps use the shared boarding point. Its real-world geographic inspiration is [Tesla's Fremont factory at 45500 Fremont Boulevard](https://www.tesla.com/findus/location/deliverycenter/fremontdirectydelivery); there is no real launch site implied. The original procedural rocket is a stylized [Starship/Super Heavy](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship/design) interpretation; its proportions are approximate game art. Fremont opens at level 5. The site is off the I-880 corridor rather than a supported autonomous destination; drive the last stretch manually.

The Oakland approach ramp rises 4 m between x=370 and x=398 on z=180; its shared slope supports cars and launches them from the lip. The orange Oakland stunt loop is a 12 m radius, 12 m wide vertical ribbon centered at (450, 180); its analytic surface is shared by collision and rendering. Rocket fans adhere on contact, including the ceiling, independently of tank air. Other vehicles collide with its inside surface; its outside also blocks airborne approaches. This is a simplified arcade collision, not a general mesh solver.

The fictional Rocket Dome replaces the ballpark at the compressed China Basin waterfront, centered at (−50, 205), south of the Bay Bridge. A shared plaza extends the SF shoreline and clears overlapping city blocks; a road from the z=120 city street leads into the marked north gate. Its 150 m-long floor, curved capsule walls and 50 m ceiling are shared by rendering, ball collision and vehicle support. Vehicles can drive in and out through the north gate; Rocket-capable vehicles can adhere to the walls and ceiling using `domeS`. The shell admits side fly-ins that fit inside; descending from above lands on the shared exterior roof. Direct travel returns players to a checked SF or island spawn based on where they entered; the ferry serves North Bay Playground separately. Stadium architecture and statues are decorative, like other street furniture. The location and baseball-inspired plaza refer to the [Giants' Oracle Park](https://www.mlb.com/giants/ballpark) and its [Willie Mays entrance and statues](https://www.mlb.com/giants/ballpark/services-and-locations), not a surveyed reconstruction or an affiliated venue.

The 24 charging lots use compressed fictional placements and shared footprints. Four marked bays at each site serve as the fictional air-refill zone and as parking-game stalls. The generated city buildings clear these footprints, while the roads remain unchanged; the charger posts are decorative. Site identities and source links are in `shared/stations.ts` and are not real-world layout or charging availability.

- Outside the four authored hills, overpass and stunt surfaces, land and bridge decks remain flat. Water is visually lower; there is no swimming, general boat navigation, tidal simulation, street elevation, accurate Bay Bridge tunnel, or exact-sized landmark geometry. The real 101/I-880 interchange is represented by a flat fictional joining point, not mapped stacked roads.

- Building envelopes are physical; trees, visual piers outside the shared ferry aprons, cranes, rails, cables, distant hill backdrops, billboard posts and charger posts are decorative. Do not create barriers on a playable road without matching shared collisions. Trees must stay clear of road segments.
- Any expansion of coastline, slopes, bridge ramps or obstacles must update shared geometry, minimap, simulation tests, and client/server together. Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` for incompatible world interpretation so stale clients reconnect. Preserve both crossings, charge sites, parked and NPC fleet, accessible spawn points, and mobile performance.
- Model artists may replace `src/bay-world.ts` visuals, but must keep those contracts and provide license/provenance plus desktop and touch playtests. Do not use a downloaded map or copyrighted texture without its license and attribution.

### Existing structure audit

The unreleased structure-collision candidate extends authored collision parts to existing northern/southern landmarks, ordinary roofs, round/tapered towers, bridge supports and stadium masonry. See [STRUCTURE_COLLISION_AUDIT.md](STRUCTURE_COLLISION_AUDIT.md) for reproduced defects, current verification and remaining limits. Avoid adding bounding boxes around open structures or replacing faceted curved surfaces with an unrelated analytic cap.
