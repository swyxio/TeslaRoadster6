# Bay geography and contributor contract

The map is a compressed arcade interpretation, not navigation data or a 1:1 recreation. Relative relationships follow the [Golden Gate Bridge District](https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/bridge-operations/traffic-management/) and [Metropolitan Transportation Commission](https://www.mtc.ca.gov/operations/programs-projects/bridges/san-francisco-oakland-bay-bridge): Golden Gate links San Francisco and Marin across the strait; the Bay Bridge links San Francisco and Oakland via Yerba Buena Island, with a western suspension section and an eastern span. Local streets, coastlines, widths, elevation and landmark positions are artistic approximations.

## Bay circuit and source

`HIGHWAYS` traces northbound [US 101 relation 108619](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/108619) and [I-880 relation 74660](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/74660), joined by fictional city connections at SF, San Jose and Oakland. The source extract in `shared/bay-route-source.json` records relation, way and node versions and coordinates retrieved 2026-10-17. The I-880 relation had one approximately 66.9 m gap between nodes 12433107327 and 1688870550; the game joins those endpoints with a straight interpolation. Regenerate `shared/bay-highway-traces.ts` with `python3 shared/bake-bay-highways.py`.

The bake projects coordinates approximately to meters, then independently rotates and scales each corridor to fit the existing game-city anchors (roughly 1:6.8–1:6.9) and initially simplifies it with a 35 m game-space tolerance. It removes one sharp Oakland kink, rounds inland bends, and replaces the abrupt San Jose meeting point with a broad fictional connection; these artistic changes can move the final road beyond that simplification tolerance. Both traffic directions share a fictional 32 m-wide surface; lane counts, destination labels, district skylines and shorelines are authored for play. They do not represent exact locations, ramps or carriageways. The closed SF → Peninsula → San Jose → East Bay → Oakland → Bay Bridge route measures about **19.86 game km** along `BAY_CIRCUIT`, including the city connections. The two city street turns are right angles, so slow down there. In the earlier protocol-v5 Bay build, a controlled test starting the San Jose connection at 160 or 220 km/h stayed within the road in both directions; an approach at full 259 km/h boost left the pavement. Those observations do not verify the merged Rocket physics. I-280, the other Bay crossings and real grade-separated interchanges are not implemented.

The first-party code and hand-authored map content remain covered by the repository's MIT license. The SF Kart data pipeline informed the approach; no SF Kart assets are included.

`shared/map.ts` is the single authority for bounds, land polygons, road segments, bridge decks, parks and collision buildings. Positive X is east and positive Z south. `isSurface(x,z)` defines ground coverage; `supportHeight` and `supportNormal` describe its height and slope as well as the crossing, stunt ramp and building roofs. `supportId` distinguishes ground, overpass, stunt ramp, stunt loop, roof and airborne entities. Loop height and orientation depend on `loopAngle`, since a vertical loop has two possible heights at one x/z; generic x/z height and normal lookups return null for the loop. Most land and both Bay crossings remain flat; a small authored off-road hill south of SF and a fictional overpass crossing US 101 demonstrate shared elevation. The overpass approaches connect to the surrounding ground, not to an interchange. The visible water surface is Y=−4. Grounded vehicles stop at unsupported shores; an airborne entity landing in open water returns to a free spawn with air and condition preserved. `nearbyBuildings` is the shared spatial collision index. Physics, solo and Workers import the same data; clients never decide a landing or a hit.

`src/bay-world.ts` builds the landscape from those definitions, with instanced roads, buildings and windows. `src/main.ts` builds its minimap from the same polygons and roads. `shared/traffic.ts` consumes the shared Bay route in addition to city loops. Parked vehicles are collision-checked on creation; the first available spawn faces east toward the Bay Bridge. Tesla charger identities remain real, while compressed placement and air service are fictional. Nearby Oakland's charger is on the Oakland landmass.

The Oakland approach ramp rises 4 m between x=370 and x=398 on z=180; its shared slope supports cars and launches them from the lip. The orange Oakland stunt loop is a 12 m radius, 12 m wide vertical ribbon centered at (450, 180); its analytic surface is shared by collision and rendering. Rocket fans adhere on contact, including the ceiling, independently of tank air. Other vehicles collide with its inside surface; this is a simplified arcade collision, not a general mesh solver.

- Outside the authored hill, overpass and stunt surfaces, land and bridge decks remain flat. Water is visually lower; there is no swimming or boat physics, tidal simulation, street elevation, accurate Bay Bridge tunnel, or exact-sized landmark geometry. The real 101/I-880 interchange is represented by a flat fictional joining point, not mapped stacked roads.

- Building envelopes are physical; trees, piers, cranes, rails, cables, hill backdrop and charger posts are decorative. Do not create barriers on a playable road without matching shared collisions. Trees must stay clear of road segments.
- Any expansion of coastline, slopes, bridge ramps or obstacles must update shared geometry, minimap, simulation tests, and client/server together. Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` for incompatible world interpretation so stale clients reconnect. Preserve both crossings, charge sites, parked and NPC fleet, accessible spawn points, and mobile performance.
- Model artists may replace `src/bay-world.ts` visuals, but must keep those contracts and provide license/provenance plus desktop and touch playtests. Do not use a downloaded map or copyrighted texture without its license and attribution.
