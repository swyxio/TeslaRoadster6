# Bay geography and contributor contract

The map is a compressed arcade interpretation, not navigation data or a 1:1 recreation. Relative relationships follow the [Golden Gate Bridge District](https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/bridge-operations/traffic-management/) and [Metropolitan Transportation Commission](https://www.mtc.ca.gov/operations/programs-projects/bridges/san-francisco-oakland-bay-bridge): Golden Gate links San Francisco and Marin across the strait; the Bay Bridge links San Francisco and Oakland via Yerba Buena Island, with a western suspension section and an eastern span. Local streets, coastlines, widths, elevation and landmark positions are artistic approximations.

## Bay circuit and source

`HIGHWAYS` traces northbound [US 101 relation 108619](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/108619) and [I-880 relation 74660](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/74660), joined by fictional city connections at SF, San Jose and Oakland. The source extract in `shared/bay-route-source.json` records relation, way and node versions and coordinates retrieved 2026-10-17. The I-880 relation had one approximately 66.9 m gap between nodes 12433107327 and 1688870550; the game joins those endpoints with a straight interpolation. Regenerate `shared/bay-highway-traces.ts` with `python3 shared/bake-bay-highways.py`.

The bake projects coordinates approximately to meters, then independently rotates and scales each corridor to fit the existing game-city anchors (roughly 1:6.8–1:6.9) and initially simplifies it with a 35 m game-space tolerance. It removes one sharp Oakland kink, rounds inland bends, and replaces the abrupt San Jose meeting point with a broad fictional connection; these artistic changes can move the final road beyond that simplification tolerance. Both traffic directions share a fictional 32 m-wide surface; lane counts, destination labels, district skylines and shorelines are authored for play. They do not represent exact locations, ramps or carriageways. The closed SF → Peninsula → San Jose → East Bay → Oakland → Bay Bridge route measures about **19.86 game km** along `BAY_CIRCUIT`, including the city connections. The two city street turns are right angles, so slow down there. In the earlier protocol-v5 Bay build, a controlled test starting the San Jose connection at 160 or 220 km/h stayed within the road in both directions; an approach at full 259 km/h boost left the pavement. Those observations do not verify the merged Rocket physics. I-280, the other Bay crossings and real grade-separated interchanges are not implemented.

The source extract and generated highway geometry are © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the [Open Database License 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/); see [OpenStreetMap copyright](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). The first-party code and hand-authored map content remain covered by the repository's MIT license. The SF Kart data pipeline informed the approach; no SF Kart assets are included.

`shared/map.ts` is the single authority for bounds, land polygons, road segments, bridge decks, parks and collision buildings. Positive X is east and positive Z south. `isSurface(x,z)` defines ground coverage; `supportHeight` and `supportNormal` describe its height and slope as well as the crossing, stunt ramp, island bank, island oval and building roofs. `supportId` distinguishes these surfaces, the stunt loop, island cliff, dome interior and exterior roof, ferry and airborne entities. Loop and cliff height and orientation depend on `loopAngle`, and dome support on `domeS`, since their surfaces can have more than one height at one x/z; generic x/z height and normal lookups return null for those surfaces and the moving ferry. Most land and both Bay crossings remain flat. In addition to the original off-road hill south of SF, three small authored, drivable hills evoke Twin Peaks, Mount Tamalpais and Mission Peak; a fictional overpass crosses US 101. The ground mesh, sampled heights, slopes and vehicle support share the same terrain triangles. These features are arcade-scale interpretations, not real elevation data. The overpass approaches connect to the surrounding ground, not to an interchange. The visible water surface is Y=−4. Grounded vehicles stop at unsupported shores; an airborne entity landing in open water returns to a free spawn with air and condition preserved. `nearbyBuildings` is the shared spatial collision index. Physics and Workers import the same data; clients never decide a landing or a hit.

`src/bay-world.ts` builds the landscape from those definitions, with instanced roads, buildings and windows. `src/main.ts` builds its minimap from the same polygons and roads. `shared/traffic.ts` consumes the shared Bay route in addition to city loops. Parked vehicles are collision-checked on creation; the first available spawn faces into San Francisco toward the initial delivery. Tesla charger identities remain real, while compressed placement and air service are fictional. Nearby Oakland's charger is on the Oakland landmass. Four shared charger-lot footprints provide marked parking activities and Rocket pickups. Their positions and air refills are fictional game placements; their stall geometry is shared by the simulation and renderer.

Rocket Island is a fictional, oversized playground north of San Francisco. Its shared polygon spans about 1,400 × 1,120 game meters, including the island ferry apron; `isRocketIsland` covers that polygon for safety checks at any altitude. Its loop and dock road use shared road geometry. `FERRY_DOCKS` records both apron tips and the centers for a 38 m deck. The SF apron joins the x=0 street; the island apron joins the loop. Water separates the two and the island remains separate from Marin and the Golden Gate. `MAP_BOUNDS.minZ` is −2,400 for the island. These positions are game fiction, not real island or ferry geography.

The island has two authored stunt surfaces in `shared/island-stunts.ts`; `src/island-stunts.ts` renders their exact shared vertices. The east–west bank runs from x=−20 to 180 at z=−1,750. Its 28 m lane rises across its width, tapers to ground at both ends, and has sloped shoulders. Ground driving and swept landings follow its triangulated height and normal; jumping uses that normal. The 24 m-wide cliff ribbon starts at (420, −1,850), curves upward with a 20 m radius, and extends to a 225° overhang. Cars enter eastbound from its foot; an ordinary car detaches at the vertical, while an active Rocket can follow the overhang. Jumping releases the car with its world velocity. These are bounded arcade surfaces, not a general terrain or adhesion solver.

The speed oval uses shared triangles from `shared/oval-bank.ts` for physical support, normals and swept landings; `src/island-stunts.ts` renders the same lattice with a small vertical offset to avoid flicker. Its 28 m lane banks outward, with shoulders tapering to ground. The start/grid, short-course overlap and dock junction stay flat, blending into the bank over 70 m. The existing oval centerline remains the race and minimap route. Steering and traction remain arcade behavior rather than a realistic banking model.

The landmarks add stylized, collision-backed versions of [Stanford's Hoover Tower](https://community.stanford.edu/opportunities/hoover-tower), [UC Berkeley's Campanile](https://visit.berkeley.edu/campus-attractions/campanile), [SFO's control tower](https://www.flysfo.com/about-sfo/awards/new-control-tower-sfo-honored-top-engineering-project-america), [Fremont's Mission San José](https://www.fremont.gov/government/departments/economic-development/real-estate-development-investment/historic-districts/better-block) and the [San José City Hall rotunda](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/event-services/facilities-and-fees/rotunda). `LANDMARKS` in `shared/map.ts` owns their game-space footprints and clear arrival points. Their dimensions and placements are invented, and SFO's runway is a driving area with no aviation simulation. Lake Merritt's miniature islands are inspired by its [bird islands](https://www.oaklandca.gov/Community/Parks-Facilities/Parks/Lakeside-Park); the lake and islands are decorative over drivable ground. Larry’s Imaginary Yacht is a fictional Easter egg, not a depiction of Larry Ellison's real yacht or its location.

Curved roofs for the arena, San Jose rotunda, Coit and Salesforce caps, and selected northern domed landmarks follow their shared arcade surface approximations. Other landmark collisions retain simple building envelopes, so they are not a general mesh solver.

The 100 additional places comprise 50 northern and 50 Peninsula, Fremont and South Bay identities in `shared/north-landmarks.ts` and `shared/south-landmarks.ts`. Of these, 99 have shared building collision envelopes; Mission Peak uses terrain instead. Twin Peaks and Mount Tamalpais add two named summit markers. They supplement the pre-existing landmarks above. Original low-poly models use recognizable silhouettes and nameplates, but the redistributed sites are not surveyed or exact-sized. Their sources are indexed in [northern references](BAY_LANDMARKS_NORTH.md) and [southern references](BAY_LANDMARKS_SOUTH.md).

`shared/billboards.ts` places decorative, non-colliding signs beside US 101 and I-880, using 12 original designs for entirely fictional parody companies. The placement checks supported ground and clears road surfaces, buildings and gameplay markers; their posts are decorative. They are not real advertisements or sponsors.

In the fictional 2050 setting, `TESLAND_MONUMENTS` places the Elon Colossus and First Delivery Monument on the Marin headlands, clear of the Golden Gate approach. Their rectangular bases use the shared building collision and roof support, and the minimap uses their shared positions. The figures, car and plaques above the plinths are decorative and do not collide. They are original in-game satire, not real landmarks or affiliated installations.

`FREMONT_SPACEPORT` defines a fictional factory, launch pad, tower and clear boarding ring near the compressed Fremont district. Factory, pad and tower are shared building envelopes; the rocket, tower arms and signs are decorative. Both maps use the shared boarding point. Its real-world geographic inspiration is [Tesla's Fremont factory at 45500 Fremont Boulevard](https://www.tesla.com/findus/location/deliverycenter/fremontdirectydelivery); there is no real launch site implied. The original procedural rocket is a stylized [Starship/Super Heavy](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship/design) interpretation; its proportions are approximate game art. The site is off the I-880 corridor rather than a supported autonomous destination; drive the last stretch manually. The South Bay opens at level 2.

The Oakland approach ramp rises 4 m between x=370 and x=398 on z=180; its shared slope supports cars and launches them from the lip. The orange Oakland stunt loop is a 12 m radius, 12 m wide vertical ribbon centered at (450, 180); its analytic surface is shared by collision and rendering. Rocket fans adhere on contact, including the ceiling, independently of tank air. Other vehicles collide with its inside surface; its outside also blocks airborne approaches. This is a simplified arcade collision, not a general mesh solver.

The fictional Rocket Dome replaces the ballpark at the compressed China Basin waterfront, centered at (−50, 205), south of the Bay Bridge. A shared plaza extends the SF shoreline and clears overlapping city blocks; a road from the z=120 city street leads into the marked north gate. Its 150 m-long floor, curved capsule walls and 50 m ceiling are shared by rendering, ball collision and vehicle support. Vehicles can drive in and out through the north gate; Rocket-capable vehicles can adhere to the walls and ceiling using `domeS`. The shell also admits fly-ins that fit inside; above-roof overflights remain outside. Direct travel returns players to the Bay or island from which they entered; the ferry serves Rocket Island separately. Stadium architecture and statues are decorative, like other street furniture. The location and baseball-inspired plaza refer to the [Giants' Oracle Park](https://www.mlb.com/giants/ballpark) and its [Willie Mays entrance and statues](https://www.mlb.com/giants/ballpark/services-and-locations), not a surveyed reconstruction or an affiliated venue.

The four Supercharger sites use compressed fictional placements and 24 × 24 m shared lot footprints. Four marked bays at each site serve as the fictional air-refill zone and as parking-game stalls. The San Mateo lot is placed near the compressed Peninsula corridor, clear of the stadium. The generated city buildings clear these footprints, while the roads remain unchanged; the white/red charger posts are decorative. Site identities and source links are in `shared/stations.ts` and are not real-world layout or charging availability.

- Outside the four authored hills, overpass and stunt surfaces, land and bridge decks remain flat. Water is visually lower; there is no swimming, general boat navigation, tidal simulation, street elevation, accurate Bay Bridge tunnel, or exact-sized landmark geometry. The real 101/I-880 interchange is represented by a flat fictional joining point, not mapped stacked roads.

- Building envelopes are physical; trees, visual piers outside the shared ferry aprons, cranes, rails, cables, distant hill backdrops, billboard posts and charger posts are decorative. Do not create barriers on a playable road without matching shared collisions. Trees must stay clear of road segments.
- Any expansion of coastline, slopes, bridge ramps or obstacles must update shared geometry, minimap, simulation tests, and client/server together. Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` for incompatible world interpretation so stale clients reconnect. Preserve both crossings, charge sites, parked and NPC fleet, accessible spawn points, and mobile performance.
- Model artists may replace `src/bay-world.ts` visuals, but must keep those contracts and provide license/provenance plus desktop and touch playtests. Do not use a downloaded map or copyrighted texture without its license and attribution.
