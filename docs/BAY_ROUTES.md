# Bay route names and geography

Tesland's roads and attractions are a compressed arcade interpretation. These references establish real place and route identities, not the geometry, distances, access ramps, building scale, or driving rules in the game. The traced highway provenance and fictional joins are documented in [MAP.md](MAP.md).

| Real route | Primary-source reference | In-game interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US 101, Bayshore Freeway | [Caltrans' named freeways](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/research-innovation-system-information/documents/place-names/web-2022-named-freeways-final-a11y.pdf) lists the Bayshore Freeway from San Jose to San Francisco. [Caltrans' corridor plan](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/district-4/documents/d4-transportation-planning-local-assistance/system-regional-planning/f0020333_us-101-south-ccp-a11y.pdf) places it through Palo Alto, the Peninsula and San Jose. | The `peninsula` highway is a transformed, simplified OSM trace. The San Jose connection is fictional. |
| I-880, Nimitz Freeway | [Caltrans](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/district-4/documents/880-interchange-improvements/88-improvements-consolidated_master-v68.pdf) identifies the freeway as a north–south connection from Oakland through Fremont to San Jose. Its [named freeways list](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/research-innovation-system-information/documents/place-names/web-2022-named-freeways-final-a11y.pdf) supplies the Nimitz name. | The `east-bay` highway is a transformed, simplified OSM trace, with an authored connection at each end. |
| I-80, San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge | The [Metropolitan Transportation Commission](https://mtc.ca.gov/operations/programs-projects/bridges/san-francisco-oakland-bay-bridge) identifies the bridge as I-80 and describes its spans via Yerba Buena Island. [Caltrans' exit list](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/safety-programs/documents/exit/f0017912-80.pdf) lists the Oakland connection to I-880 and University Avenue in Berkeley. | The current flat deck and city approaches are authored approximations; the island tunnel, real ramps and divided decks are not modeled. |
| I-280, other crossings | [Caltrans](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/district-4/documents/d4-transportation-planning-local-assistance/system-regional-planning/tcrs/f0020254_i280draft_final_tcr_signed_07162013_nr_ig-a11y.pdf) identifies I-280 as a San Jose–San Francisco corridor. Its [US 101 plan](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/district-4/documents/d4-transportation-planning-local-assistance/system-regional-planning/f0020333_us-101-south-ccp-a11y.pdf) identifies the Dumbarton and San Mateo bridges. | These are references for future roads, not currently modeled routes or promised exits. |

The roadside route markers label the existing US 101, I-880 and Bay Bridge surfaces. They do not indicate exact exits, lane configurations or navigation directions. Never add an I-280 or other bridge sign until the corresponding shared drivable route exists.

## Place references

- [SFO](https://www.sfomuseum.org/aviation-museum-library/hours-directions) describes access directly from Highway 101. The airport location and facilities in the game are artistic approximations.
- [Stanford](https://theory.stanford.edu/seminar/directions-gates.html) describes US 101's University Avenue exit and University Avenue becoming Palm Drive toward campus. [Stanford's campus map](https://campus-map.stanford.edu/) is the reference for identifying campus places.
- [UC Berkeley](https://www.berkeley.edu/map/) places its campus north of Oakland and identifies Sather Tower and Sather Gate.
- [The City of Fremont](https://www.fremont.gov/about/attractions) describes Central Park, Lake Elizabeth and its attractions.
- [The City of San José](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/cultural-affairs/special-events/venues-contacts-resources/venues) identifies Plaza de Cesar Chavez in downtown.
- [San Francisco Recreation and Parks](https://sfrecpark.org/941/GGP---Strawberry-Hill-Picnic-Area) describes Strawberry Hill as an island within Blue Heron Lake in Golden Gate Park. A decorative lake without a shared water boundary does not yet provide the physics of an island.

Landmark names establish geographic inspiration, not affiliation, endorsement or exact real-world appearance. Fictional Easter eggs are fictional; do not imply the real-time location or presence of a real person's property.
