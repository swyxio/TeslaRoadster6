# Sky currents

Fly into a blue moving lane to gain speed in its arrow's direction and replenish some air. A lane slows an unpowered descent. Golden rising rings mark thermals: jump into one to extend the jump and refill some air. Both work automatically for any airborne vehicle, including a stolen car without a rocket kit; a kit or Roadster helps reach them. Neither holds an unpowered car aloft indefinitely. Grounded cars and people are unaffected. Air is capped at 100%, and currents do not alter collision, police or delivery rules.

The low City Glide lane is 12 m above the Bay approach and can be reached with two jumps. Four higher city lanes sit at 30, 38, 62 and 150 m. Separated northbound and southbound skyways sit at 55 m over portions of the fictional 101 and 880 corridors. Each Caltrain stop also has a short, narrow pair of northbound and southbound lanes centered at 45 m; these are separate local flight features, not a continuous train service. The single thermal near the Transamerica Pyramid starts 2 m above the road and rises to 96 m; its center is offset from the landmark collision box. The high Skyline Express takes deliberate air jumps and flight to reach, and cars have a 180 m flight ceiling. The minimap shows blue dashed arrows and a gold ring; world labels identify each current, the instrument panel identifies it when occupied, and an entry notice calls out the refill. These locations and heights are static for discoverability and deterministic multiplayer, not a live or random weather feed.

`shared/sky-currents.ts` is the source of truth for all volumes and `currentAt`. The shared simulation applies the rules in solo play and authoritative rooms, and the renderer and HUD read those definitions; clients do not claim a current. Overlapping volumes resolve to a thermal first, then the first matching lane. Physics replenishes 1 air unit per second in thermals and 2 in lanes, converted to a percentage of the installed tank's capacity; lanes accelerate at 3 m/s² along their direction up to a 60 m/s target without reducing existing faster motion, and ease an unpowered descent. Thermals provide 2.5 m/s² of upward acceleration toward a 6 m/s pre-gravity target. Regular Rocket thrust can overcome a lane without boost. Within 10 m of a locked progression region in the wind direction, or when already in one, the lane still refills air but no longer pushes or assists the car vertically, and airborne checkpoint collisions preserve vertical and permitted lateral movement. This allows a driver to leave or fall away even with an empty tank. All values are arcade tuning. Visual markers are replaceable and have no collision authority.

The National Weather Service describes [onshore winds through the Golden Gate](https://www.weather.gov/media/mtr/baybreezes/spotter_newsletter_fall09_web.pdf) and [thermals from surface heating](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/turbulence_stuff/turbulence/turbulence.htm). These inspired the location choices; real thermals are weather-dependent, and tower locations do not imply guaranteed updrafts. These low-altitude arcade lanes are not real atmospheric jet streams. Air magically replenishing in wind is fictional. [Coit Tower's location](https://sfrecpark.org/Facilities/Facility/Details/Coit-Tower-290) and all landmark geometry are compressed game interpretations.

A future glider upgrade could trade powered lift for longer unpowered travel and reward finding thermals; it is not implemented or part of the current protocol.
