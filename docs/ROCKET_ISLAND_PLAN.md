# Rocket Island and parking playgrounds — proposal

## The experience

A deliberately oversized, fictional Alcatraz-inspired **Rocket Island** is a free-roam physics playground: no police or tickets on the island, with an inverted cliff, banked speed course, jumps, refill points and optional races. Drive or jump a car onto an autonomous ferry at San Francisco; watch the crossing and drive off at the island. A temporary Rocket pickup makes any of the 26 playable vehicle families capable of the existing fictional Rocket moves. Every authored parking lot in the world is also an optional playground for parking, queue-cutting and jumping games. The map is imaginative game geography, not a model of the real island or a claim about real vehicle capabilities.

The enclosed Rocket Dome soccer arena is implemented with direct, reversible travel; the ferry, larger island, speed oval, inverted cliff, races, upgrades and parking games described below remain a design. Keep the normal Bay loop, delivery, police, fleet and solo/multiplayer game playable at each stage. No new accounts, permanent upgrades, purchases or persistent leaderboard are needed.

The dome has one shared ball and blue/orange goal counts without assigned teams. Either side reaching five wins, followed by an automatic match reset. Rocket Roadsters can adhere to its curved walls and ceiling. Arena travel is immediate for now, not an autonomous ferry. Delivery order time pauses and police pursuit clears while in the dome.

## Space and first-pass math

- Prototype an island approximately **1,400 × 1,100 game m**, north of SF. One candidate footprint is x=−500…900, z=−2,200…−1,100, with the SF dock near (0, −215) and the island dock near (100, −1,100). Extend `MAP_BOUNDS.minZ` to about −2,400. These are planning coordinates: check the final polygons, dock clearances, sight lines, shoreline and ferry swept path against SF, Marin, bridges, buildings and spawn sites before choosing them.
- Allocate a roughly 1,200 × 800 m speed oval, a separate short circuit across its interior, and a cliff/jump zone with safe runouts. A stadium shape with 400 m-radius ends and 400 m straights is about 3.31 km around. At a constant 160 or 220 km/h this would take about 75 or 54 seconds respectively, **before** acceleration, braking, collisions or imperfect driving. A short roughly 1 km route makes a solo lap practical even for slower cars; neither distance is a measured course.
- Today's steering clamp implies roughly 10 m/s² maximum lateral acceleration: at 220 km/h an ideal turn needs radius ≥373 m, and at 259 km/h ≥518 m (`v²/r`). A 400 m bend is an initial target for roughly 200–220 km/h, not a guarantee; 259 km/h needs a larger bend or braking. Banking may change this after actual physics work. Mark curves and tune from tests rather than promising flat-out boost everywhere.
- The example ferry path is about **891 m**. With an illustrative 20 m/s cruise, 3 m/s² acceleration/deceleration and 20 seconds docked at each end, a one-way trip takes about 51 seconds; one ferry's departure cycle is about 142 seconds. Assuming uniformly timed arrivals and no queue, mean wait to departure is about 71 seconds, up to about 142 seconds after just missing one. With six cars, its theoretical maximum is roughly 152 cars/hour per direction; player demand, blocking and real throughput are unmeasured. If the wait feels poor, test a second ferry with separate passing lanes/docks, or shorten the path; don't speed up world physics.

Use the existing **48-minute day / 30× displayed day clock** only for the sky and demand. Ferry motion, waits, race timers, pickup duration, parking games, physics and active delivery time use ordinary simulation seconds. A paused solo simulation and an empty/restarted room do not accrue wall-clock progress; communicate that the room is ephemeral.

## Ferry: one shared moving surface

Start with one visible, autonomous, deterministic ferry in the same authoritative world as both shores. It follows a fixed, obstacle-checked route and owns its state: docked at SF → boarding → crossing → docked at island → boarding → return. Show destination, occupancy and a countdown on dock signs, minimap and an accessible HUD. The deck is a physical shared support surface, not an animation or a hidden teleport. Its transform, velocity, gates and exact dock times are server-owned and replicated; render, collision and audio follow that state in solo and multiplayer.

A first deck target is **38 × 14 m**, with six 10 × 5 m vehicle spaces (two lanes of three, leaving approach/end margins). The longest current catalog tractor is about 7.7 × 2.55 m; verify all 26 actual collision footprints, entry turning circles and safe boarding with multiple cars before fixing the size. Provide separate marked foot spaces so an on-foot player can board. A supported, low-speed approach ramp lets cars drive **or jump** onto a stationary open deck. No boarding a closed, departing or moving ferry in the first slice; a failed jump lands or recovers under normal water rules. While docked, cars remain driveable and collide; a player can leave before departure.

At departure, accept eligible occupied spaces in stable arrival order, with a deterministic ID tie-break. Clearly show when a car is secured; its pose is attached to the moving deck and driving/exit controls are temporarily unavailable for the crossing, while camera and menu remain usable. This avoids slip, collision impulses and lag throwing players into water; it is a conscious first-version tradeoff. There is no fee or selection of another vehicle. Open the gate on arrival and let passengers drive or walk off. After a displayed timeout, place remaining riders at a reserved, collision-checked local dock apron, preserving vehicle, driver, health and air. Reserve enough protected apron slots for a full ferry; never place a passenger into another body or solid. If an apron is blocked, hold the affected rider/ferry safely, offer recovery and retry rather than overlapping bodies or dropping anyone in water.

Define disconnect, reset and exit as atomic server transitions. A disconnected passenger is detached and removed using existing owner/NPC cleanup rules; an abandoned NPC car stays safely aboard until it can be placed at a clear destination, then rejoins ordinary NPC behavior only when supported by a valid route. A reset during a crossing removes the rider from the manifest and uses a checked shore spawn; it cannot duplicate a car, block a slot or strand another passenger. A new room member observes the current ferry and occupants, not a local replay. At a full deck, leave the waiting player at the dock with clear next-departure information. Test multiple boarding/disembark orders, two departures, empty ferry, overflow, recovery, disconnect and reconnect, and blocked docks.

Implementation requires an explicit moving `supportId` and deck-local attachment/velocity, dock surfaces and land/water collision queries in the shared simulation, plus snapshot state for ferry/occupants and area-of-interest inclusion for the ferry, its passengers and nearby players. Current supports are static; current `isSurface`, landing, `fits`, reset, exit and NPC route code cannot simply treat a moving deck as ground. Passengers must remain visible to nearby clients even while their underlying ambient entity would normally be culled. Bump the wire protocol for incompatible state and update `docs/CONTRACTS.md`, live room tests and clients together.

## Island physics and the no-police boundary

Create the island polygon, docks, roads, banks, cliff and recovery spots as shared map geometry consumed by simulation, minimap and renderer. Keep a visible water gap; the ferry is real travel, not a bridge. Add checked island recovery and fleet-selection spawns so an island reset or a vehicle change does not silently return the player to SF. A water fall near the island recovers locally if a clear spot exists; elsewhere keep normal recovery. The existing 100 m altitude cap, water recovery, collision envelopes and every station/delivery site need explicit regression coverage.

The oval can start as a **20–30 m wide banked ribbon** with smooth entry/exit transitions. A separate inverted cliff can start as a 24 m-wide, roughly 20 m-radius arc sweeping past vertical to about 225°: its peak is about 40 m above its base, within the current altitude cap. Give it a broad landing/runout area and a flat beginner bypass. These are starter dimensions, not tested clearances.

The current ground path keeps cars upright, the static hill is a height field, and the Oakland loop is a special parameterized vertical circle; a visual mesh alone cannot create a drivable bank or overhang. Add bounded, authored support surfaces with stable IDs, local coordinates, a position and normal, explicit entry/exit transitions, swept contact and consistent renderer geometry. Use tangent velocity and surface normals for jumps, local gravity, grip and orientation. An overhang cannot be represented by `height(x,z)` alone; don't select a surface by x/z when two heights overlap. Extend collision/landing and vertical filtering enough for the ribbon, including fast crossing and two cars on different levels. For the first version, Rocket-capable cars use fictional fan adhesion on the inverted section; other cars follow gravity and can fall or bounce. All cars can use the ordinary banked lane. On detachment, preserve tangent/world velocity and hand control back to existing airborne physics. Do not promise a general-purpose 3D rigid-body, tire or suspension solver.

A single authoritative island safety polygon applies at every altitude, including above the cliff; ferry passengers are also protected while attached to the ferry. The server clears an existing pursuit on entering either protection area, prevents new detection, slowing or tickets there, and sends the cops back to patrol. Previous tickets are not refunded. When a player leaves the polygon/deck, normal detection resumes; no client-supplied location or UI toggle grants immunity. A flight across open water is not automatically protected. Do not spawn police on the island. Free roaming retains physical collisions, recovery and accessible exits; racing and parking rules apply only to players who opt in.

Pause the current **order's** pickup/delivery countdown while a player is on the island or attached to the ferry, and show “delivery paused”; resume with the same remaining time on return. The separate active-play/leaderboard clock still follows its existing rules. This avoids punishing exploration while preserving cash, current order and payout authority. No island restaurant or automatic delivery is introduced in this slice.

## Optional racing

Place a prominent start marker and an accessible Race action at the dock and track. Default to a short one-lap time trial; offer the speed oval as a second course. One shared lobby per course allows 1–8 players, opens for 15 simulation seconds, then displays a 3-second start countdown. A solo player can start immediately; spectators and free roam continue without joining. All 26 cars and active Rocket upgrades are eligible. Participants remain physical and can bump each other; the screen must say so before joining. There is no cash stake or claim of a fair esports race.

The server assigns a valid starting slot, records start time, and advances an ordered sequence of wide 3D gates when the car's swept path crosses each gate in the intended direction. Gates include a generous height window so jumps work, but crossing only in x/z far above a gate cannot score. Checkpoints prevent simply skipping a lap; legitimate airborne shortcuts between them remain part of the playground. The finish counts only after all gates, once per entrant. Rank by authoritative elapsed simulation time, breaking equal times deterministically by player ID. Display progress, wrong-way/missed-gate feedback, finish time and results for 30 seconds. Close a race when all finish or after 300 seconds; unfinished, disconnecting, vehicle-switching or resetting entrants are DNF. Leaving the lobby before the start is allowed. Do not let a client send finish times, checkpoints, placement or rewards. No persistent leaderboard is implied.

Replicate only bounded course/lobby/entrant state and route join/leave intent through validated, rate-limited protocol messages; don't add an unbounded event log. Test simultaneous starts, gate tunneling at maximum speed, reverse crossings, skipped gates, high jumps, finish ties, collisions, late join, disconnect, reset and race timeout. Confirm the slowest catalog car can complete the short circuit within the timeout under normal play; adjust route or timeout after a real test.

## Temporary Rocket pickups for any car

Add visually distinct, authoritative Rocket pickup locations on the island and at accessible parking playgrounds. Driving through an available pickup grants the **current vehicle entity** Rocket capabilities for an initial **90 simulation seconds**, fills its existing air tank to 100, and shows a countdown. The pad becomes available after 20 simulation seconds. Simultaneous claims use a deterministic winner; reacquiring refreshes to 90 seconds from the new pickup, with no stacking. A person cannot collect it. The stock Rocket Roadster already has its permanent capabilities; a pickup can refill its air without making those capabilities expire.

Keep model identity, dimensions, mass, standard handling and ownership. Add an entity-level expiry/capability resolved in the server simulation; never mutate the shared vehicle registry, rely on mesh type, or trust a client claim. While active, grant the same fictional boost, jump, flip, hover, roll and surface-fan behavior used by the Roadster, with the existing 0–100 air resource and boost limits. Upgrade effects survive ferry travel and leaving the island until expiry. Show thrusters and the timer to local and remote players for every mesh, including large trucks. Leaving/switching/removing a vehicle does not transfer the upgrade to another one.

On expiry, preserve current position and momentum, stop new Rocket thrust and safely detach from any inverted fan support into ordinary gravity; never teleport, refill or let a vehicle remain stuck to a ceiling. Stock Roadster capability remains. The current `spec.airThrusters` gate, Roadster-only visual emitters, ground/air controls, loop adhesion and fleet switching all require deliberate changes. Existing acceleration/thruster tuning is arcade and does not account for mass in a realistic way; measure and tune all 26 vehicles rather than suggesting heavy trucks physically perform like real Rockets. Verify pickup contention, refresh, expiry midair/on an overhang/on the ferry, disconnect, vehicle change, all 26 IDs and multiplayer presentation.

## Every parking lot is a playground

There is no authoritative registry of existing playable parking lots today. Add one shared list of lot footprints, entrances, markings, clear driving space and stall/queue positions, then make **every lot in that list** support the same optional playground controller. Audit existing map blocks rather than assuming decorative spaces are usable; resize/reposition conflicting buildings or select an appropriate nearby footprint, with a safe route and no interference with streets, chargers, deliveries, player spawns or bridges. Newly added lots must use the same definition. Each lot has a Rocket pickup and a clearly visible, reachable start/exit marker. Initial template: a roughly 70 × 55 m lot with an approach, at least four 10 × 5 m stalls and runout; tailor to collision-checked space and the largest vehicle, not a fixed promise about current geometry.

Two opt-in activities share the same space:

1. **Park it:** a random marked stall lights up. Drive or jump into it and stay below 1 m/s with the whole body inside, within 20° of the slot heading, for 2 seconds. The server starts/stops the clock, checks actual body geometry and awards a time. Cars remain physical; a crowded stall is part of the game. Allow solo or up to eight entrants, with separately highlighted targets where practical.
2. **Cut the queue:** players enter a marked approach in timestamp order; optionally add a few clearly marked stationary NPC cars for solo play. Once the start signal appears, reaching an eligible empty parking stall before someone who entered ahead of you scores a playful overtake. Jumping over or around the queue is allowed; no invisible teleport or forced movement. Track order and each claimant on the server, prevent double-scoring one arrival, break simultaneous events deterministically, and cap a round at 90 seconds. Leave the queue or reset to withdraw. This is an arcade challenge, not a rule imposed on ordinary parking.

Lot play never blocks through traffic or converts another player's car to a bot. Cops remain normal at mainland lots; the no-police boundary is the island/ferry protection area. Keep a free-roam bypass and exit, readable signage, non-overlapping pickup/activity markers and low-cost, reusable visual instances. Test every registered lot for a supported clear footprint, all vehicle sizes, inaccessible/blocked stalls, simultaneous claims, jumping, leaving, disconnecting, spectator collisions and cleanup.

## Controls, rollout and acceptance

Desktop uses a visible nearby action with a keyboard equivalent for ferry/activities, plus existing steering, jump, flip and hover. Touch has the same labeled actions, held/tap behavior and a reliable exit; keep driving controls visible in portrait and landscape. Provide text/status for ferry wait, full deck, attached controls, Rocket timer, delivery pause, race gates and parking results; no hover-only interaction or reliance on color. Preserve menu, reconnect, fleet and room-sharing flows.

Build in playable slices, with estimates only as planning ranges:

1. **Island and ferry** (roughly 1–2 weeks): shared island/docks, physical boarding and transport, safe recovery and island no-police rule. Validate 26 car footprints, solo plus two-client crossing and disconnections before proceeding.
2. **Rocket pickup for all cars** (roughly 3–6 days): capability expiry, all fleet controls/effects and edge cases. Keep the Roadster permanent.
3. **Bank and inverted cliff** (roughly 1–2 weeks): shared surface/normal collision and adhesion, then tune and visibly playtest. Do not replace the working ferry with an unfinished terrain system.
4. **Short race and parking templates** (roughly 1–2 weeks): authoritative optional activities, then reuse the parking controller across each audited lot. Add the long oval after the short course is fun.

These estimates exclude unknown device, networking and tuning costs and overlap; they are not commitments. Each slice needs `pnpm test`, `pnpm build`, protocol/integration tests for network changes, replay/determinism and physics edge tests, plus actual Chrome desktop and portrait/landscape touch-emulation playtests of entry, control and exit. Record dated evidence in `docs/VERIFICATION.md`; emulation is not a physical-device test. Run measured multi-client and same-host room-capacity checks with ferry passengers and island activities, profiling authoritative CPU, snapshot size/frequency and mobile rendering before claiming any budget or scaling improvement. No actual performance result is claimed by this plan.
