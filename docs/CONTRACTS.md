# Implementation contracts · v51 candidate

These contracts are the handoff for other coding agents. The lead integrator owns changes to shared schemas, physics, transport and deployment. A visual/UI task must not silently modify them. Dated local, Preview and production observations are in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md). Planned features are labeled separately.

Dependency flow: UI/input → session → authoritative simulation; snapshots → presentation. Presentation never writes the authoritative world. Multiplayer is server-authoritative. A connection failure must never start a local simulation.

## Coordinates and time

- Right-handed world, meters and seconds. X east/right, Y up, Z south; forward is **−Z**.
- Entity `x,y,z` is its support-center pivot, not mesh center. `y` is absolute world altitude; do not infer grounded state from altitude. `supportId` identifies ground, overpass, stunt ramp, stunt loop, island bank, island oval, island cliff, island jump, dome, dome roof, ferry, building roof or null while airborne. Loop and cliff support require `loopAngle`, and dome support requires `domeS`, because x/z alone cannot identify their heights; the ferry is a moving support. Authored building/structure roof IDs use `roof:<building-index>:<part-index>` to distinguish stacked authored surfaces; unprofiled rectangular buildings use `roof:<building-index>`. A vehicle can remain supported while its body overlaps a part's edge.
- `yaw`, `pitch` and `roll` are radians; pitch is rotation around local X and roll around local Z. Positive yaw turns left: forward vector `(-sin(yaw),0,-cos(yaw))`. Positive steer means left.
- `speed` is signed forward meters/second; UI converts to km/h by multiplying 3.6. `vx`, `vy` and `vz` are world velocities; pitchRate, yawRate and rollRate are angular rates. Air/health are bounded 0–100.
- Shared simulation uses fixed 0.05-second outer steps, subdivided for fast relative motion. Airborne speed is capped at 80 m/s (288 km/h) to keep collision sampling bounded; this is an arcade limit, not a general continuous-collision guarantee. `World.time` is simulation seconds, not epoch time. Never use render delta directly for physics. `dayMinute(World.time)` derives a fictional 48-minute day; do not accelerate physics, following gaps or signals with it.
- `shared/map.ts` owns bounds, land polygons, roads, bridge decks, surface heights/normals, Oakland stunt and dome geometry and `BUILDINGS`; `shared/island-stunts.ts` owns the separate bank and cliff geometry and contact functions, `shared/oval-bank.ts` owns the banked oval surface, and `shared/island-jumps.ts` owns island ramp geometry and contacts. See [MAP.md](MAP.md). `isSurface()` identifies ground coverage; shared support data distinguishes stacked surfaces. Water is rendered at Y=−4; unsupported water landings return a car to a checked free spawn. Avoid visual obstacles in roads without shared collision. Charger posts, trees, bridge furniture and distant hills are decorative.

## World schema

Source of truth is exported TypeScript in `shared/game.ts`:

- `Entity`: id, kind (`VehicleId | person`, 26 ambient families plus six Vault vehicles in `shared/vehicles.ts`), x/y/z, yaw/pitch/roll, vx/vy/vz, angular rates, speed, health/air (percentage), tankLevel (1–5), stunt count, loop attachment, support identity, dome membership, return destination (`domeReturn`) and surface position, authoritative thruster-effect bitmask and per-vehicle `upgrades` (`rocket`, `economy`, `motor`, `armor`, `autonomyJump`, `autonomySky` booleans), driver (player ID or null), owner, pushX/pushZ (lateral velocity), steering, npc/autopilot, routeId/waypoint/pauseUntil, and current `navigation` route, mode and status; police cars additionally have `police` pursuit state.
- `Player`: id, display name, entityId, `delivery` (cash, lifetime delivery earnings and eligible income, level, last blocked access attempt, active time, order, completed runs, ticket state and OTA subscription), `achievements` (unlocked awards and server-owned flight tracking), and `cockpit` (self-reported camera selection and accumulated run statistics). Each player controls exactly one entity; each vehicle has at most one driver.
- `Pickup`: id, x/z, kind (`repair|air|rocket`), active, optional readyAt (simulation seconds).
- `World`: entities, players, pickups, ferry, parking rounds, race rounds, time, `mars` (`fund`, `roomFund`, `launchAt`) and authoritative `soccer` (ball position/velocity, both scores, goal/win phase, per-player class-specific touches and credited goals, last touch, winning player IDs and Pulse Shot cooldowns). A Rocket kit installed by pickup or purchase counts as a retrofit. Station/building, parking-lot, course, shop and island surface definitions are static versioned data, not repeated in snapshots.
- `Input`: throttle/steer/roll are finite numbers in [−1,1]; boost/hover/brake/cockpit are booleans. `cockpit` is a client claim, never an authoritative proof of view. Jump and flip are bounded integer press counters; holding the jump control sets hover after a short delay; the simulation consumes changed counters once, including when a tap is shorter than an input frame. Complete input frames replace previous intent.

Use the functions `createWorld`, `addPlayer`, `removePlayer`, `step`, `interact`, `resetPlayer`, `chooseVehicle`, `toggleAutopilot`, `travelDome`, `buyUpgrade`, `subscribeOta`, `parkingAction` and `raceAction`. Gameplay must not trust UI validation. Keep identity/ownership changes inside simulation functions. Iteration is deterministic for the same initial world, step sequence and inputs; UUID generation belongs to the server, not physics.

Welcome precedes the initial snapshot. The room simulates one fixed 50 ms outer step per completed tick and sends snapshots every two ticks. `tick=T` is the world *after* step T; it advances only when a step completes, independent of wall time. `roomEpoch` identifies one room incarnation: it changes after the room empties and resets, not when a single driver reconnects. The WebSocket is that driver's connection generation. `substeps` is the count used by the most recently completed step (1 at tick zero). The authoritative room retains the adaptive global maximum-speed substep policy. Presentation replay may override its local count, but a last-known count cannot predict unseen traffic, collisions or changing physics.

`reward` is sent only to the owner after a wallet receipt commits. Cash credited by one physical or delivery transaction is combined in its celebration. Each new reward has an increasing per-room-player ID; clients queue and display them in order so multiple commits between snapshots do not overwrite one another. The message is transient, not a durable inbox: a disconnected or identity-changing client may miss its celebration, while wallet settlement remains governed by the journal. `lastReward` in the world remains state for level tracking, not a second celebration source.

Input frames carry a strictly increasing positive `seq`, the room epoch, the driver's current `controlRevision`, the latest sent `actionSeq` (initially zero), and the intended `targetTick`. The server accepts continuous controls only for T+1 through T+8. A newer sequence replaces the same slot and supersedes older slots at later ticks, preventing old acceleration from returning after a newer release. A missing slot holds the last controls for at most six completed ticks, then neutralizes. A 300 ms wall-clock watchdog also clears stale queued and held controls on the next callback; an edge-only frame cannot refresh the continuous-control watchdog. The rejected frame gets `inputRejected`; a sequence is receipt ordering, not elapsed time or an execution acknowledgement. `sync` tags a regular snapshot after that driver's preceding economy operations settle; requests are coalesced.

Jump and flip are modulo-2^31 monotonic per-connection press counters. The scheduler recognizes new counters separately from held levels; repeated or backward counters cannot fire again. It reserves successive ticks for distinct same-kind edges, within T+1 through T+8. A slightly late edge (up to three ticks) may be rescheduled even though its continuous controls are rejected; older, far-future and overflow edges expire. Each pending edge expires after 300 ms wall time, independently of later input. `inputEdge` is private to the owner: `accepted` or `rescheduled` reports an edge bridged across an old revision before its level rejection, and a later terminal outcome reports whether it was processed, expired or superseded. Counter gaps do not invent intermediate presses. The simulation consumes an edge even if the car is secured on the ferry or the action is otherwise ineffective; an edge invalidated before the relocated car consumes it is superseded.

Every potentially invalidating action carries a strictly increasing positive `actionSeq` and a synchronous `edgeBaseline` of the current counters; an invalid or backward baseline closes the socket. The server records the sequence even when cooldown or game rules reject the action, but only an accepted action cancels pending controls and marks its baseline seen. Input continues while `sync` waits. After an accepted action, an input with an older revision may still schedule new edges if its action sequence matches the latest processed action and its revision is at least `edgeFloorRevision`; its levels are rejected. Autonomous water or ferry relocation raises the floor to the new revision, closing that bridge even after later actions. A wrong room epoch is rejected before modifying the scheduler. Clients capture separate presses in a bounded queue rather than inferring them from a counter gap.

The owner-only `control` field has `revision`, `actionSeq`, `edgeFloorRevision`, `receivedSeq`, `appliedSeq`, `held`, `heldUntilTick`, `queued` (at most eight `{seq,targetTick,input}` slots) and `jump`/`flip` (`seen`, `processed` and at most eight pending `{id,targetTick}` entries). `receivedSeq` means an ordered frame for this epoch was received, including rejected frames; `appliedSeq` identifies the most recent continuous slot actually run. It is not a duration. The snapshot's T makes all steps through T final; clients replay only later ticks. Reset attempts, accepted teleport/minigame/race placement, vehicle takeover or replacement, autopilot/destination changes, water recovery and forced ferry-apron relocation clear that driver's queue and increment `control.revision`. The separate top-level `poseRevision` increments when the controlled entity changes or its pose changes by more than 1 mm or 0.0001 radians, or on a reported water/ferry relocation. This prevents an autopilot change or floating-point noise during a no-op reset from forcing a camera cut. A revision change for one driver never resets another's. Socket closure clears only that driver's session.

Each snapshot retains all players, occupied vehicles and ferry-supported entities plus ambient entities within 1,400 m of the recipient; distant ambient entities may appear/disappear as the recipient moves. Shared fields and individual entities are serialized once per broadcast tick before assembling each recipient's complete filtered JSON. Each player is serialized at most once per owner and remote projection; collection awards are present only in the owner's projection. Command queues and edge metadata are sent only to their owner. The server alone owns collisions, position, pickups, races, cash and HUD state. Prediction is presentation only and must reconcile authoritative snapshots, including unknown contacts and special surfaces. The client closes/reports incompatible protocol versions. Wire changes require coordinated client/server updates, a version bump and integration tests; do not add compatibility shims for this prototype.

Sky-current volumes are static versioned world data in `shared/sky-currents.ts`; they are not sent in snapshots. The simulation decides membership and applies lift, speed and air. See [SKY_CURRENTS.md](SKY_CURRENTS.md).

After welcome, clients send `identity` with a recovery code or `null` for a guest. The server pauses that player's controls, progression, pickups and station or Dome air refills until the decision and any recovery resolve, without holding the rest of the room. A paused driver does not block another driver from collecting a pickup. Existing physics and world-time subscription periods continue during recovery. An initial guest decision is accepted in socket order; a saved-driver client waits for the matching result and its subsequent snapshot before enabling play. The server validates visits, shop discounts and purchases; clients cannot claim visits or set prices.

The map includes a static, collision-backed monument base in the Marin headlands. Its footprint and landable roof height come from `TESLAND_MONUMENTS` in `shared/map.ts`; the sculpture above it is decorative. Rendering and minimap markers use the same position.

Names, Dome travel, mini-game entry and mini-game start/leave bypass the action cooldown but remain subject to the socket rate limit and simulation validation. Both conditions are retryable and distinguishable from a connection failure. Missing Origin is permitted for public nonbrowser clients; Origin checks are not authentication.

Upgrade, style, OTA, identity and Vault requests carry a positive safe-integer request ID; corresponding results are sent only to the requester, including cooldown rejections where applicable. After a saved recovery is requested, a null identity choice leaves the driver paused until rejoin. The UI must wait for the matching result and show timeouts without claiming success. OTA subscription state and billing use authoritative game time and delivery cash, charging $100 per game hour (120 real seconds); remote purchases require an active subscription and a driven vehicle, while physical shops remain available without it. Subscriptions enabled before the rate change retain their paid remainder and then stop; the driver must re-enroll to accept the new rate. Re-enrolling during the paid remainder preserves it and charges $100 at the next renewal.

Both destination requests have an action fence, a required `mode` (`road`, `jump`, `sky`), and a private `destinationResult`. The server validates the purchased per-vehicle tier and rocket capability. `deliveryDestination` routes to the room's current pickup or dropoff, with the endpoint at most 4.5 m from its marker; rooftop or unsafe/unreachable destinations are rejected. Map pins retain their normal road snapping. The driver may take over with manual controls.

Capacity: `MAX_PLAYERS`, default 64, clamped 2–128. Production and preview use 64 as a provisional admission limit, not a validated playable capacity. A full room rejects the connection with code 4003. Automatic entry tries `bay` then up to 16 public overflow rooms on a pre-welcome 4003, bounded by 20 seconds; each room makes its own authoritative admission decision. An explicit room, invite or property link stays exact and offers retry or another room. See [public placement](ROOM_OVERFLOW_PLAN.md). A named `GameRoom` owns each room’s in-memory simulation; its timer stops and world resets when empty. Per-driver `DriverProfile`, per-room `RoomEconomy` and one `GlobalIncome` Durable Object handle wallet and income accounting. Input and physics proceed without waiting on profile RPCs. See [dated isolation and performance checks](VERIFICATION.md) for the measured conditions and limits.

## Wallet and ownership transaction boundary

The server creates a guest wallet on join. `EconomySession` keeps a per-driver draft and ordered queue; `RoomEconomy` records intents and pickup reservations, while `DriverProfile` checks revisions and fences and records receipts with wallet changes. The visible wallet is the last committed result. Identity, purchase and collection requests are validated against server-owned state; a slow driver profile must not block other players' room physics or input acknowledgements. `sync` barriers wait for the requesting driver's preceding economy operations.

The profile includes balance, earned and eligible income, current order and completion count, achievement IDs, owned Vault vehicle IDs, personal vehicle upgrades/finishes, Cybercab fleet state and OTA state. Position, health, air, traffic and activity rounds stay in the room. Local checks of reconnects, identity recovery, uncertain outcomes, timer checkpoints and isolation under delay are recorded in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md). Property and player-trading scope is described below.

In the candidate, the room stages server-observed delivery stops, physical and match achievement evidence, and supply pickups in wallet order. A stage is accepted after its journal transaction and final profile-fence check, or when fence recovery adopts the already-written row. Evidence still in room memory or awaiting acceptance is volatile. A supply stage reserves its pickup generation in the same journal transaction; a successful profile receipt credits $5 once, while a terminal rejection releases the reservation without consuming it. Accepted evidence is reevaluated against committed progress after earlier operations resolve; delivery stops that no longer match are no-ops. Profile receipts commit the resulting progress and awards idempotently. Region milestones caused by delivery, physical achievements, supply or cab income commit with the triggering credit. Flight and cockpit tracking, uncommitted Mars boarding, and changes since the last periodic timer or OTA checkpoint remain volatile.

Delivery stages preserve the observed stop, active-order clock and event time. If historical rate data is unavailable, the room retains subsequent physical evidence and defers that driver's dependent wallet decisions until it can reproject them; driving and other players continue. Cockpit statistics are session-only and the deferred-payout tick can be omitted from their bookkeeping. The per-driver queue is bounded at 32, with a possible final ticket beyond that check; overload disconnects that driver for recovery. Cab stages have a separate eight-stage preparation limit. A stalled acceptance or inconsistent physical receipt can block that driver's handoff; there is no automatic repair tool or fixed durability deadline.

An accepted delivery stop that has not yet settled when reward rules change is evaluated using the rules in effect at settlement, with its recorded event-time multiplier. Committed receipts retain their original amounts on replay.

## Mars mission

`shared/mars.ts` owns the $1,000,000,000 target and boarding eligibility. The `GlobalIncome` authority aggregates each driver's cumulative eligible game-created income; profile outboxes retry credits and cumulative per-driver accounting avoids double counting on replay. `mars.fund` is the global total capped at the goal; earnings count automatically without spending cash. The separate `mars.roomFund` preserves that room's earlier contributions and accepts optional new contributions. A contribution debits the player's wallet and credits only that room's fund; it never counts toward global income. The journal reserves the full request against the room limit, rejects an over-limit request without a debit, and includes the currently unreserved `available` amount in that rejection. A successful private result reports the amount applied. Retries and recovery reconcile the durable debit and credit before publishing the result.

`GET /api/starship/status` returns the confirmed global status and server time, with `personal:null`. A connected driver requests the private status over its fenced room connection; guest and recovered drivers use the same canonical profile. The client shows committed earnings awaiting global confirmation separately. `GET /api/starship/contributors?group=at-goal|later&cursor=...` pages through public names; anonymity is the default and hides amounts as well as names. Cursors are bound to the group. The at-goal list freezes confirmations at the threshold, while later confirmations can include earnings committed before it. The index may return `building` and should be retried. An already-complete pre-upgrade ledger has an unknown historical crossing, not an invented launch.

Profile income and privacy outboxes retry independently with persisted, jittered exponential backoff. Timeouts bound each caller's wait, not the lifetime of a remote operation; credits and privacy revisions are safe to replay. Private status exposes whether a privacy choice is still pending so the client does not mistake the previously confirmed name for the final choice.

At a newly confirmed goal crossing, the global coordinator stores a launch ID and a launch time 30 seconds later. The world construction and playback derive from the confirmed total and server-time anchor. Polling and network transit can introduce presentation delay; this is not frame-synchronized. Replay is available after completion and never starts another launch or changes earnings. No season or room is retired.

A completed Dome match pays each present participant who touched the ball $250, plus $500 for each winning participant. Tied winners all receive the win award; spectators and players who leave before the result do not. Those repeatable awards use the confirmed rate at match completion, while one-off and tier achievement awards remain fixed. The match result and wallet receipts are journaled so retries and reconnects do not pay twice. A match timestamp ahead of the receiving economy object's clock is retried without changing the recorded time or disconnecting the player. A verified Dome win with a player winner starts a ten-minute 1.5× room bonus, refreshing on a subsequent win; its window begins at least one millisecond after the result and is published after its awards settle. A match without a player winner starts no bonus. An authenticated operator may schedule a 2× game-wide event for 30 minutes, at least 60 seconds ahead; combined activity pay is capped at 3×. Delivery pickup and drop-off components use their authoritative payout-time rate; dispatched cab income is settled proportionally across complete historical windows. A missing or stale event lease defers settlement until the historical rate is confirmed while driving continues. A lease issued slightly ahead of the room callback clock waits asynchronously within the existing five-second observation budget. The room then rechecks its win generation, pending-win barrier and exact lease interval; it never extends an expired lease. Refresh failures emit a bounded server diagnostic without driver or wallet data.

Operators configure a high-entropy `STARSHIP_ADMIN_TOKEN` secret (at least 32 characters), then `POST /api/admin/earnings-events` with `Authorization: Bearer <token>` and JSON `{"requestId":"<unique-id>","startsAtMs":<future-UTC-ms>}`. A repeated request ID with the same start is idempotent; conflicting IDs, overlap and insufficient lead time are rejected. Never commit or log the secret. Without it, scheduling returns 503.


Once either the global total or this room's fund is fully funded, a player must be on the ground within 14 m of the shared Fremont boarding point, with both forward speed and lateral push magnitude below 1 m/s, to request boarding. The server records the `to-mars` achievement with the driver profile, broadcasts `launchAt` in simulation time, and returns a private result after the award commits. Additional drivers joining an active 12-second launch do not restart it; a later eligible driver starts a new launch. The cinematic launch and victory presentation are client-side, and free roam continues. There is no playable Mars surface.

## Global chat and saved identities

Same-origin WebSocket `GET /api/chat` connects to a single global chat Durable Object, independent of game rooms. The chat client sends `{type:"hello",name:string,code?:string}` first, then `{type:"message",text:string}`, `{type:"rename",name:string,requestId:number}`, `{type:"save"}`, or a `{type:"ping"}` heartbeat. The server confirms a rename privately with `{type:"renamed",playerId,name,requestId}`; rename failures echo the request ID. Per-connection messages retain their order with respect to name changes, using the name that was current when each message was processed. Accepted work drains after disconnect, and a saved identity cannot reconnect until it has drained. Message times represent broadcast time. The server answers `{type:"ready",playerId,name,messages}`, broadcasts `{type:"message",message:{id,playerId,name,text,time}}`, and privately returns `{type:"recovery",code}` for a successful save or `{type:"error",message}` when a request fails. An active saved identity is reported with `code:"identity_active"`; the client retries briefly before requiring action. Heartbeats receive no reply. It derives author IDs and names from the connection, not from message fields.

Names are NFKC-normalized, stripped of control and invisible direction/format characters, whitespace-collapsed and capped at 20 Unicode code points; an empty name becomes `Driver`. Active names are compared case-insensitively. A conflict adds the first available ` #2`, ` #3`, and so on, trimming the base if needed. Names are not reserved when disconnected. Game rooms use the same rule independently, so a room display name can differ from the global chat name. Room labels are self-asserted: they are not proof of a saved chat identity. The full server-issued UUID identifies a chat sender; a short on-screen prefix is for convenience and is not guaranteed unique.

The client can save a new driver and remember its recovery code in browser storage; storage or quota failures are shown to the player. Saving generates a random 256-bit bearer secret; the code contains the public chat player UUID and secret. Chat stores the SHA-256 verifier, saved name and associated server-created wallet ID. A valid code is sent privately to the room to bind the guest wallet or recover an existing saved wallet. Chat atomically records both the identity-to-wallet binding and a reverse wallet claim, so competing requests cannot attach two identities to one wallet; retrying a bound identity returns its existing wallet. A guest with progress must rejoin to switch to a different saved driver. An unbound identity also requires rejoining when the current wallet is already claimed by another identity; it then binds to the new guest wallet. The profile stores cash, delivery progress, awards, owned Vault vehicles, selected vehicle configuration and OTA state; room positions, live health and match state remain ephemeral. Anyone with the code can recover the identity, so never put it in URLs, broadcasts, history or logs. Losing the code loses access. See [verification](VERIFICATION.md) for local recovery and fencing checks.

A first binding starts with independently tracked provisional claims. A stale or disconnected request releases its own claim; a confirmed claim survives a lost reply and may be recovered with the same code. When browser storage is available, the client stages a new code before sending the bind and keeps any previous saved code until confirmation. A staged code is retried after reload when it is the only saved code; otherwise the identity panel offers it alongside the previous driver. If staging fails, the room still receives the active chat identity and pauses for its result. The identity panel displays the code and a persistent warning to copy it; an earlier unfinished code is preserved. Respect the remember preference and show storage failures.

Chat messages are normalized and limited to 240 Unicode code points. The server accepts at most five messages per connection per 10 seconds and limits all chat requests. The in-memory hub permits at most 512 sockets, 24 simultaneous sockets and 60 connections per IP per minute, and 30 new saved identities per IP and 240 globally per hour; missing IPs share an unknown bucket. Connections inactive for three minutes are closed; the client sends a heartbeat every 25 seconds. These limits reset on object restart and are modest abuse brakes, not a capacity measurement or comprehensive moderation. At most 50 recent messages are kept in memory and supplied on join; a runtime restart clears them. Chat sockets currently keep the object awake while connected. Reconnect and failure states must remain visible; chat failure must not silently change room play. Render names and messages as text, never interpolated HTML.

## Fleet and autonomy contract

`shared/vehicles.ts` defines model identities, dimensions, wheelbase, mass, steering, acceleration, braking, stock capabilities and provenance. Renderer and simulation consume the same IDs and dimensions. Estimated dimensions and mass must remain labeled; all handling is arcade tuning. The `roadster` has stock fictional Rocket capability. A free pickup grants a session kit; a purchased kit on a personal vehicle is included in the candidate saved configuration. Air jumps, hover, adhesion and tank performance are game fiction. Suction operates only on supported stunt surfaces.

Collision bodies are oriented rectangles using registry length/width; mass-weighted impulse response includes lateral velocity. Bicycle steering uses wheelbase and bounded steering angle, with quicker, tighter steering for manual drivers; Cybertruck/Zoox have enhanced low-speed steering and visually countersteering rear wheels. A grounded player car moving forward can drift by steering with brake; lateral momentum decays after release. Opposing throttle brakes to a stop before reversing and suppresses steering while changing direction. Powered airborne throttle damps opposing and sideways velocity to make aiming more responsive. This is not suspension/tire simulation. Terrain roof support remains shared.

Each room starts with eight parked cars and eight city NPCs sampled from the 26-model ambient catalog. An explicit fleet seed selects the cohorts; the server chooses a new seed on room creation/reset, while pure simulation fixtures default to seed zero. All ambient models retain takeover, autonomy and manual driving coverage across cohorts. The six Vault IDs are excluded from ambient traffic and free fleet selection. Their purchase is location- and wallet-validated, and collection requires confirmed ownership at the Vault or a physical workshop. Another player cannot take an unoccupied luxury car. `npc` is identity; `autopilot` is current control mode. NPCs have null `driver`, so on-foot players may take them; taking one disables autonomy and stops it. Human-occupied vehicles cannot be taken. Exiting or disconnecting releases an NPC back to street autonomy after a pause when it can attach to a route. Other empty vehicles remain parked unless explicitly enabled.

Autonomy uses deterministic right-lane loops, including both directions around the Bay, speed-dependent following gaps and a timed eastbound Bay Bridge approach signal. Traffic demand follows the accelerated day, while the signal and driving behavior use real simulation seconds. The free-roaming cruise toggle engages on ground within 9 m of a supported street route; destination requests can also plan a checked off-road join. A player's throttle/steer/brake/jump/flip/boost/hover/roll input immediately cancels AV mode. The server owns route state and motion; renderers never advance traffic. No real AV/vehicle API is used. Mode is available for every model as game fiction, including historical prototypes.

The expanded map can request a destination. `shared/navigation.ts` builds a directed graph from the existing traffic lanes and checks unlocked regions. A collision-checked local join can reach a connected lane from supported off-road ground; unsafe or inaccessible joins are rejected. The follower makes bounded reverse attempts when progress stalls, then reports failure instead of teleporting. Water, disconnected island roads, locked areas, unreachable lanes, airborne starts, rooftop goals and excessive detours remain unavailable. The server records route, selected mode and driving/arrived/failed status, and clears stale manual inputs on success. Manual input cancels the route. Double-tapping Shift or using the touch-accessible delivery button requests the current order target through the same fenced action.

Street Pilot (`road`) is included. Ludicrous Leap (`jump`) and Maximum Plaid (`sky`) are paid per-vehicle upgrades requiring rockets, and Plaid also requires Leap. Leap may hop a stopped leader when its approach and landing are clear; Plaid may fly along a checked corridor and use a favorable thermal or jet with an exit. They consume the normal fictional rocket air and otherwise fall back to ground driving. Upgrades are applied only after the wallet commits and follow a saved driver's personal vehicle model; older saved vehicle records retain existing upgrades and default new tiers to unowned. These are bounded arcade heuristics, not a guarantee for every traffic situation.

Ambient demand targets two to four unattended autonomous highway cars alongside eight city NPCs and four police. Taken cars, off-route abandoned cars and cars reattached to city routes do not count toward that target; the optional ambient pool is capped at 12 in total, so replenishment can be delayed when its reserve is full. At most one ambient car is added or removed every five simulation seconds. Removal is beyond 1,600 m from every player and preserves occupied and stopped autonomous cars; hidden off-route abandoned cars and free-flowing city-route cars may be reclaimed when the pool blocks replenishment. Available hidden spawn sites and safe removal conditions also limit how quickly traffic follows demand.

Fleet selection accepts only Tesla IDs, requires supported speed≤1 m/s and is unavailable on the ferry or while attached to an arc. Other families remain drivable through street takeover. It releases the old vehicle, creates the requested player-owned model at a checked free spawn, and removes abandoned personal duplicates. IDs are strict allowlisted own registry properties (not `in` checks susceptible to prototype names). No client-supplied mass, coordinates or damage. Names bypass the action cooldown but remain rate-limited so startup selection isn't lost behind a name message. Protocol version constant is `PROTOCOL_VERSION` in shared/game.ts; incompatible clients explicitly reload.

Geometry and materials in the renderer are shared static caches. Removing an entity must not dispose shared geometry. Four-wheel assemblies are named `wheel_fl`, `wheel_fr`, `wheel_rl`, `wheel_rr`; bikes use `wheel_f` and `wheel_r` plus a rider group. The renderer uses authoritative steering, hides unoccupied riders and adds grounded visual lean. `jets` contains independent rear, corner and side cold-gas vapor groups, instantiated for other models when upgraded. The simulation publishes a thruster bitmask. Vehicle, traffic and multiplayer checks remain required.

## Rocket Dome soccer

`shared/dome.ts` defines the fictional China Basin waterfront arena and its north entrance; `shared/map.ts` supplies its land, plaza, street approach, capsule surface and support basis. `travelDome` moves one player and current vehicle or person between the arena and a checked Bay or island spawn, preserving health and air and returning them to the last supported Bay or island side (or the ferry destination). Cars can drive in from the street and out through the marked north gate without teleporting; vehicles can also enter through the sides once their upright bodies fit. Descents from above land on the exterior roof and overflights remain outside. Goal apertures contain cars. The arena has no police detection or citations, and pauses the current delivery order clock while leaving the active-play clock intact. The protocol requires matching world geometry and soccer match state on both ends.

Gold arrows mark the air-charge zone: a Rocket-equipped vehicle attached to the dome at least 8 m above its floor and moving at least 5 m/s refills air at 24 percentage points per simulation second, capped at 100. It also works on the ceiling and while boosting, including with a Rocket kit from a pickup; stationary, airborne, floor-level and non-Rocket vehicles cannot charge. The shared simulation owns the change and clients display replicated air and eligibility.

`shared/soccer.ts` owns the one room-wide ball and colored scores. Any car can hit either goal; there are no player teams. Crossing a colored goal awards that color a point, freezes play for three simulation seconds, then returns the ball to center. The first color to five ends the match; after a six-second winner display, both scores, player match stats and the ball reset. The last driven car to physically strike the ball earns the goal for its driver and its class; an undriven car's strike clears the last touch. The player(s) present in the dome at match completion with the most credited goals win; if no eligible player has a credited goal, the match has no player winner. Leaving and returning before the finish retains the player’s match goals. Departing the room removes that driver's match stats and pending touch. Physics, scoring and timing are authoritative in online rooms; clients render snapshots and never send ball positions, goals or score claims. A new or empty room starts a new match.

The first credited win unlocks `dome-champion` and Champion Charge; a credited goal restores 25 air, capped at 100, to the scorer's present rocket-equipped car. Both abilities are Dome-only, use committed achievements, and need no new controls. They remain inactive while a driver's identity is attaching. Pulse cooldowns are room-local and removed on departure; the unlocks follow the saved driver.


## Cybercab and market

Only a connected saved driver can purchase a Cybercab. The server generates the asset ID and price; the profile commits the debit, asset and one-time ownership award with its receipt. Paint uses the existing server-side workshop catalog and is committed to that specific owned asset. Repeated request IDs on one connection are coalesced while pending or replayed after completion; older evicted IDs are rejected. After an uncertain disconnect the UI waits for the same saved wallet to reconnect and reconcile before allowing a new purchase. The wallet fence drains and evicts an earlier session before binding the next one.

`dispatchCybercab` only uses committed ownership. Its provisional budget is eight additional cab entities per room, including borrowed retired copies; the allocator shares slots across owners, keeps occupied cars, and settles before retiring an unoccupied car. A dispatched cab retains its owner through takeover. When its owner leaves, an occupied borrowed car loses its durable asset identity and becomes a temporary ride until released. The cab's `assetId` is opaque and the owner receives its entity outside the normal ambient-interest radius. The fleet dialog exposes owner/driver, committed gross income, paint and map find/follow. The cap is not a measured room-capacity guarantee.

The room measures consecutive observed wall-clock ticks only while the owner is active and an owned cab is dispatched, autonomous and unoccupied. It does not infer operation across gaps longer than 200 ms. It closes intervals after one second of observed activity or on pause/disconnect and stages them in the room journal with a stable ID and a contiguous per-fence stage ordinal. Acceptance requires a committed journal row and a successful final profile-fence check, not merely the one-second checkpoint: an open interval and any stage queued or awaiting acceptance remain volatile. The journal row can be removed if that fence check finds the session superseded. A stalled profile-fence check can extend the window, so there is no fixed elapsed-time durability bound. Accepted batches recover after a room restart and drain in order before a fence handoff; profile receipts atomically advance each cab's non-overlapping watermark, fractional remainder, cash, eligible income and one-time $2,000 award at $20,000 gross. On wallet-queue or eight-stage preparation backlog overload, the room closes and queues the final observed intervals for staging, stops accrual and disconnects without projecting the tail through the speculative wallet. Unresolved rejected or out-of-order staged records retain their evidence and block the driver's handoff; there is not yet an automatic or operator repair tool. A slow driver's storage does not block other players' physics. Accepted stages are serialized per driver so a later interval cannot be durably accepted while an earlier stage is uncertain.

After a fence is drained and sealed, the room journal retains a small closed-fence marker to reject delayed producers that passed an earlier profile check. Completed markers are not alarm work; storage grows by one marker per completed fence until a safe compaction policy is designed.

The market uses a single durable registry for server-minted cosmetic, collectible and completed-home instances, fixed-price listings and operation IDs. The room identifies the saved driver; the client cannot choose a wallet or seller. Prices are nonnegative whole game-cash units. A sale debits the buyer, credits the seller `floor(price × 95 / 100)` and removes the remainder; it does not count as game-created eligible income. Property acquisition requires $2 million immediately before the transaction and a free purchased-home slot. The initial city-supplied $1 million home is an instanced visitable exterior; plots, building, garages and improvements remain design work.

Cross-object settlement is a recoverable coordinator protocol, not one atomic database transaction: the registry durably records the operation, locks the affected asset, prepares and seals each driver's existing profile wallet, applies idempotent wallet writes, publishes custody, then releases the wallets and asset. A completed result is sent only after releases; an uncertain operation stays pending and recovery retries it. An active participant must be stopped in a safe personal car outside shared activities; otherwise the operation can reject as busy. While held, that player is excluded from physics and wallet accrual, and other drivers continue. Client storage stages the operation ID and command before sending; after reconnect, status is checked and a not-found operation is resent with the same ID. Failure to write or read the journal blocks new actions. Public and owned inventories paginate separately. Equipped market paint is profile-owned, rendered on personal vehicles, and must be unequipped before listing.

A completed home has a stable canonical address derived from its asset UUID, independent of its current owner. Only canonical property addresses use registry lookup; other valid room names, including ordinary `p-` names, remain ordinary rooms. A property room verifies that asset with the registry on entry and checks safe arrival positions near the shared collision-backed house. The owner label is public presentation, not an authorization credential; registry unavailability fails entry closed. No interior, garage or transferable improvements are represented by this home.

Before settling an accepted cab interval, the room-owned history barrier checks its durable pending-win journal, and the economy consumes all relevant room and global history pages. Unavailable or incomplete history defers settlement; a current global lease cannot establish an earlier interval's multiplier. While history is pending, other wallet actions can proceed without the unavailable credit. The resolved windows are saved with the stage, then the credit is projected into the draft when it enters the ordered wallet queue and receives its wallet sequence. Subsequent actions use that projection; the visible wallet shows committed results. If an earlier wallet action is rejected, an accepted cab stage is reprojected from the restored wallet. Fence recovery drains recorded wallet intents before settling remaining stages in ordinal order. The profile commits the receipt, watermark, remainder and wallet together. Cumulative eligible income continues through the shared outbox after a Starship launch; a late confirmation may represent money earned before launch.

## Citations, rooftops and achievements

`shared/police.ts` measures airborne duration and peak height in the authoritative simulation. When a pursuit ends in a citation, `delivery.lastTicket` records the amount, offense number, flight duration, height and duration, height and repeat components. The stop and grace period are immediate; the candidate economy flow publishes the fine after its durable result. Presentation must make a citation conspicuous and require an accessible acknowledgement; clearing controls does not stop server heartbeats. A room continues running while a dialog is open. Session-local UI may not create tickets or alter their amount.

`shared/service-areas.ts` supplies the cash-level boundaries and map-overlay polygons. The same level applies on roads and in flight. Access can relock after spending, but saved achievements and ownership remain. Orders retain their numeric pickup and customer indices, including while a wallet stage is pending; existing venue IDs and stop positions must remain at their original indices. A shared spatial index limits delivery-stop evidence checks to nearby cells and preserves the catalog-order signature.

`shared/delivery-scheduler.ts` selects deterministic nearby jobs when a previous run completes or a locked order is rerouted. It favors distinct consecutive venues and alternates exploration toward newly opened tiers with local jobs. Ground pickup-to-dropoff legs are validated against the shared road planner. Drivers away from the road graph are localized to a supported road for assignment only; this does not assert that self-driving can start from their current position. Relocking a collected order retains its dropoff stage and does not pay pickup twice. A ferry-separated playground driver receives a mainland assignment and a ferry notice. If no new candidate is safe after a completed run, the completed pair is offered again with an explanation. If an inaccessible active order has no safe replacement, its stage and timer are preserved. The HUD asks the driver to return to an open road, and the room retries at most once per five simulated seconds while on open ground with no wallet operation pending. A recovered level also makes the original order available again.

Rooftop customers in `shared/delivery.ts` select actual flat `BUILDINGS` from `shared/map.ts`; the objective marker uses their shared height. A rooftop dropoff requires the car to land and stop within 6 m of the roof center. After Oakland unlocks, an eligible non-exploration order after every third completed run starting with the first may select a rooftop only when the vehicle has rockets and at least a Level 3 tank. Other vehicles receive ground orders. A saved or active roof job is rerouted to a ground customer if the driver enters a less capable vehicle; leaving the car on foot alone does not reroute it. A collected order retains its stage, elapsed time and fare ceiling. The room captures roof eligibility at delivery and citation event time; citations also capture whether the driver was on foot, deferring the vehicle check while still rerouting newly locked regions. Older staged evidence without these fields is conservatively ineligible. Repair waits while a prior delivery decision is unresolved.

`shared/achievements.ts` owns unlocked award IDs and flight tracking. Bay Hopper requires leaving one mainland shore, crossing water, and arriving above or on the other without an intervening landing; a bridge, island, roof or stunt-loop contact ends the attempt, and recovery or vehicle switches cancel it. Flying above a bridge is allowed. Roof Rider requires a car to land on a building roof after a supported departure and at least 0.35 seconds of flight with 2 m of rise or 6 m of horizontal travel. A reset, vehicle switch, large relocation or grounded contact clears the flight. Sky Delivery requires a completed rooftop delivery; Neighborhood Regular requires three deliveries. Dome awards require a real ball touch and the player to be in the dome at match completion. Rocket Rookie and Built to Fly require touches in Roadster · Rocket and a retrofit respectively; Roadster Champion and Garage Champion require a win and at least one goal credited in the corresponding class. No Rockets Needed requires a win with at least one stock-car goal and only stock-car touches. A tie counts as a win for each leader. Flight samples are server-only; snapshots carry a neutral tracker value.

Perfect Park and Queue Cutter require the corresponding parking completion; Checkered Flag requires finishing a race, and First Across requires finishing first in a race that started with at least two drivers, even if other starters do not finish. The ranked first finisher earns it immediately. The original badges themselves add no cash. The authoritative simulation submits awards through the same per-driver wallet transaction as other achievements; only committed IDs are reported as saved. Guest progress can be attached when saving that driver. The awards screen hides a recovering wallet until its identity is ready.

`shared/achievement-tiers.ts` defines fixed, capped 1 / 10 / 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 ladders for deliveries, order pickups, early pickups and dropoffs, rooftop deliveries and landings, Bay crossings, both parking modes, race finishes and contested wins, qualifying Dome matches and wins, and supply pickups. `selectAchievementTierLadder(id)` returns the five targets and additional tier bonuses for display. An early stop is strictly before that stage's estimate. A Dome match requires a recorded touch; a win also requires a credited goal. Race and parking events are captured at their completed transition, never from repeated results frames. Each accepted event advances each applicable family at most once. Discoveries, purchases, finite collections and Mars remain one-off goals.

Vehicle collections record server-observed models in the owner-only wallet. For each of the 11 Tesla and six Vault models, 100 m of horizontal movement above 2 m/s must occur in one uninterrupted ride; switching or leaving the vehicle, ferry travel, wallet binding, a relocation or a gap over 12 m in one room tick resets the partial distance. Selecting a car is not a drive. Partial distance remains room-local and is lost on disconnect; the completed model is journaled. A theft records a catalog model only after a successful on-foot takeover of an ambient NPC or server-seeded parked fleet car, using its pre-entry state; police, occupied cars, player-owned or abandoned personal cars, and owned or retired Cybercabs do not count. NPC autonomy need not be active. Tesla milestones at 1/5/11 distinct models pay $100/$500/$2,000; theft milestones at 1/5/10/26 distinct ambient models pay $100/$500/$2,000/$5,000. Each Vault model also has a separate $1,000 driving award, independent of its purchase award. Individual models and rewards are credited once. All 26 ambient models can occur in traffic or the parked fleet, but a room only spawns a subset at a time.

`vehicleCollections` is a bounded pair of validated unique model arrays in the driver profile and private wallet, never in world snapshots. Collection milestone and Vault-driving award IDs, and a `lastUnlock` referring to one, appear in the owner's player snapshot but are omitted from remote players' projections; existing other award IDs remain public. The owner's ordered award events are unchanged. A remote observer can infer that a hidden event occurred from a `lastUnlock` transition or a later event counter gap, though not its collection ID. Missing pre-feature progress starts empty without inferred history or back pay; malformed saved progress fails closed. The existing fenced achievement journal commits each observation, model set, unlock and cash atomically and replays it idempotently. Generic progress checkpoints cannot overwrite the collection. Sandbox and practice room hosts skip persistent collection evidence rather than relying on a client claim.

The owner-only wallet includes committed counts and claimed ranks; private counters are not replicated to other players. Profile receipt, count, rank and cash commit atomically through the fenced room journal. Duplicated event IDs replay the existing receipt. A pre-tier profile seeds the known delivery total and one occurrence proven by each relevant saved badge, marks those ranks claimed without back pay, and leaves unknown history at zero; malformed stored tier data fails closed. A generic progress checkpoint cannot overwrite authoritative counts. Each newly reached tier pays once. The delivery ladder preserves its existing 1 / 10 / 100 awards, with $50,000 at 1,000 and $200,000 at 10,000. Other ladders pay $350 / $1,500 / $5,000 / $20,000 at 10 / 100 / 1,000 / 10,000; the first rank adds $100 for families without a guaranteed cash-bearing legacy award and $0 extra for roof landings, rooftop deliveries and crossings, whose existing first awards still apply. These amounts contribute to cash and eligible income, not delivery-only `earned`.

`shared/research.ts` defines research visits and workshop discounts. Stop a car on the ground within 9 m of each landmark arrival at no more than 4 m/s: UCSF pays $250 and unlocks the Homes Lab badge; Berkeley pays $400 and unlocks 20% off efficient nozzles; Stanford pays $600 and unlocks 20% off sport motors; SFO pays $600 and unlocks 20% off air tanks. An SFO delivery adds a separate one-time $1,000 achievement. The profile records awarded IDs and adds awards to cash and eligible income, without changing delivery-only `earned`. Current cash can change region access. Clients cannot assert unlocks or prices. See [verification](VERIFICATION.md) for the tested wallet and replay paths.

### Isolated playtest rooms

Reserved `sandbox`/`sandbox-*` and `practice`/`practice-*` names create ephemeral
multiplayer sessions with no persistent economy or identity binding. The welcome
includes `playtestMode: 'sandbox' | 'practice' | null`; private recovery codes are
withheld by the client and rejected by the server in both modes. Sandbox alone
permits all-model fleet selection and fenced `{type:'teleport', x, z, requestId,
actionSeq, edgeBaseline}` requests, answered with `teleportResult` and a normal
pose/control revision update on success. Normal and practice rooms reject
teleport. See [Playtest rooms](PLAYTEST_ROOMS.md) for persistence boundaries,
finite unlimited-spending representation and the local isolation test.

## Structure collision authoring

See [the structure audit](STRUCTURE_COLLISION_AUDIT.md). Static `Building.parts` is shared by collision, roof support and conservative client sweeps; render descriptions supply northern/southern models, bridge towers, stadium masonry and static Fremont masses. Parts are cached at map initialization and excluded from network snapshots. A broad-phase envelope is not a wall. New bridge/arena structures use height-aware route and traffic clearance, so elevated crossbeams cannot block ground traffic. Existing main-building route/recovery envelopes remain conservative for turning room. Rooftop deliveries use the shared surface height rather than a nominal building height. Main solid roofs/decks/posts must be tagged; paint and small trim remain decorative. Variable-height parts compute top and underside extrema over the overlapping vehicle footprint; landing and continued roof support use the same contact height. The spatial index and point-height sampler are distinct from that body contact. Ordinary upright boxes retain their constant-height fast path. Changes to any support geometry require a coordinated protocol bump at integration.

Legacy structural catalogs currently locate profiles by map coordinates. North, South and East/South catalog coordinates must remain unique across those catalogs; `structure-surfaces.test.ts` checks this invariant when extending the map.
