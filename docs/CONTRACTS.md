# Implementation contracts · v30

These contracts are the handoff for other coding agents. The lead integrator owns changes to shared schemas, physics, transport and deployment. A visual/UI task must not silently modify them. They describe the current implementation, not speculative infrastructure.

| Gameplay | `shared/game.ts`, `delivery.ts`, `police.ts`, `achievements.ts`, `physics.ts`, `traffic.ts`, `ferry.ts`, `rocket.ts`, `upgrades.ts`, `island-stunts.ts`, `oval-bank.ts`, `island-jumps.ts`, `dome*.ts`, `soccer.ts`, `parking*.ts`, `race*.ts`, simulation tests | Shared map and registry | Renderer, DOM, transport |

Dependency flow: UI/input → session → authoritative simulation; snapshots → presentation. Presentation never writes the authoritative world. Multiplayer is server-authoritative; explicit solo play uses the same shared simulation locally. A connection failure must never automatically start a local simulation.

## Coordinates and time

- Right-handed world, meters and seconds. X east/right, Y up, Z south; forward is **−Z**.
- Entity `x,y,z` is its support-center pivot, not mesh center. `y` is absolute world altitude; do not infer grounded state from altitude. `supportId` identifies ground, overpass, stunt ramp, stunt loop, island bank, island oval, island cliff, island jump, dome, dome roof, ferry, building roof or null while airborne. Loop and cliff support require `loopAngle`, and dome support requires `domeS`, because x/z alone cannot identify their heights; the ferry is a moving support.
- `yaw`, `pitch` and `roll` are radians; pitch is rotation around local X and roll around local Z. Positive yaw turns left: forward vector `(-sin(yaw),0,-cos(yaw))`. Positive steer means left.
- `speed` is signed forward meters/second; UI converts to km/h by multiplying 3.6. `vx`, `vy` and `vz` are world velocities; pitchRate, yawRate and rollRate are angular rates. Air/health are bounded 0–100.
- Shared simulation uses fixed 0.05-second outer steps, subdivided for fast relative motion. Airborne speed is capped at 80 m/s (288 km/h) to keep collision sampling bounded; this is an arcade limit, not a general continuous-collision guarantee. `World.time` is simulation seconds, not epoch time. Never use render delta directly for physics. `dayMinute(World.time)` derives a fictional 48-minute day; do not accelerate physics, following gaps or signals with it.
- `shared/map.ts` owns bounds, land polygons, roads, bridge decks, surface heights/normals, Oakland stunt and dome geometry and `BUILDINGS`; `shared/island-stunts.ts` owns the separate bank and cliff geometry and contact functions, `shared/oval-bank.ts` owns the banked oval surface, and `shared/island-jumps.ts` owns island ramp geometry and contacts. See [MAP.md](MAP.md). `isSurface()` identifies ground coverage; shared support data distinguishes stacked surfaces. Water is rendered at Y=−4; unsupported water landings return a car to a checked free spawn. Avoid visual obstacles in roads without shared collision. Charger posts, trees, bridge furniture and distant hills are decorative.

## World schema

Source of truth is exported TypeScript in `shared/game.ts`:

- `Entity`: id, kind (`VehicleId | person`, 26 vehicle families in `shared/vehicles.ts`), x/y/z, yaw/pitch/roll, vx/vy/vz, angular rates, speed, health/air (percentage), tankLevel (1–5), stunt count, loop attachment, support identity, dome membership, return destination (`domeReturn`) and surface position, authoritative thruster-effect bitmask and per-vehicle `upgrades` (`rocket`, `economy`, `motor`, `armor` booleans), driver (player ID or null), owner, pushX/pushZ (lateral velocity), steering, npc/autopilot, routeId/waypoint/pauseUntil, and current `navigation` route and arrival status; police cars additionally have `police` pursuit state.
- `Player`: id, display name, entityId, `delivery` (session cash, lifetime session delivery earnings, level, last blocked access attempt, active time, order, completed runs, ticket state and OTA subscription), `achievements` (unlocked session awards and server-owned flight tracking), and `cockpit` (self-reported camera selection and accumulated run statistics). Each player controls exactly one entity; each vehicle has at most one driver.
- `Pickup`: id, x/z, kind (`repair|air|rocket`), active, optional readyAt (simulation seconds).
- `World`: entities, players, pickups, ferry, parking rounds, race rounds, time, `mars` (`fund`, `launchAt`) and authoritative `soccer` (ball position/velocity, both scores, goal/win phase, per-player class-specific touches and credited goals, last touch, and winning player IDs). A Rocket kit installed by pickup or purchase counts as a retrofit. Station/building, parking-lot, course, shop and island surface definitions are static versioned data, not repeated in snapshots.
- `Input`: throttle/steer/roll are finite numbers in [−1,1]; boost/hover/brake/cockpit are booleans. `cockpit` is a client claim, never an authoritative proof of view. Jump and flip are bounded integer press counters; holding the jump control sets hover after a short delay; the simulation consumes changed counters once, including when a tap is shorter than an input frame. Complete input frames replace previous intent.

Use the functions `createWorld`, `addPlayer`, `removePlayer`, `step`, `interact`, `resetPlayer`, `chooseVehicle`, `toggleAutopilot`, `travelDome`, `buyUpgrade`, `subscribeOta`, `parkingAction` and `raceAction`. Gameplay must not trust UI validation. Keep identity/ownership changes inside simulation functions. Iteration is deterministic for the same initial world, step sequence and inputs; UUID generation belongs to the server, not physics.

```text

{type:"state", world:World, inputAck:number, sync?:number}
```

Welcome precedes the initial snapshot. Physics runs at20Hz, snapshots at10Hz. Each snapshot retains all players, occupied vehicles and ferry-supported entities plus ambient entities within 1,400 m of the recipient; distant ambient entities may appear/disappear as the recipient moves. Shared fields and individual entities are serialized once per broadcast tick before assembling each recipient’s complete filtered JSON. Ferry, parking and race state are authoritative. Static map and simulation are shared. Input sends at20Hz with a strictly increasing positive sequence; the server keeps the latest intent and acknowledges the latest sequence actually used in a physics tick. The same input can run for multiple ticks, and intermediate sequences may be skipped. `sync` tags the next regular snapshot after preceding socket messages; multiple requests are coalesced and do not force extra snapshots or physics ticks.

For local presentation only, the client replays at most three unacknowledged inputs with shared physics from each snapshot. It stops prediction when snapshots are stale, input backlog is excessive, another body is close, the vehicle is airborne or on a special surface, or an action is waiting for its `sync` barrier. A new vehicle or discontinuity clears pending preview. The server alone owns collisions, position, pickups, races, cash and HUD state; this short preview can be corrected when the next snapshot arrives, particularly when inputs are batched or reused. Client closes/reports incompatible protocol versions. Wire changes require coordinated client/server updates, version bump when incompatible, and integration tests. Do not add compatibility shims for this prototype.

Sky-current volumes are static versioned world data in `shared/sky-currents.ts`; they are not sent in snapshots. The simulation decides membership and applies lift, speed and air. See [SKY_CURRENTS.md](SKY_CURRENTS.md).

Protocol v30 combines sequenced driving acknowledgements, local prediction, session-long Rocket pickups and the Fremont spaceport and Mars mission with the wooded North Bay, Peninsula alternatives, rebalanced sky currents and monument changes. Earlier independently developed v29 builds used the same version for different changes. The server and client must share the same world geometry, support queries and match schema; incompatible clients cannot connect. Room-full close signaling, snapshot serialization, navigation and direct minigame entry are also part of the combined release.

The map includes a static, collision-backed monument base in the Marin headlands. Its footprint and landable roof height come from `TESLAND_MONUMENTS` in `shared/map.ts`; the sculpture above it is decorative. Rendering and minimap markers use the same position. This is entirely fictional game art and does not imply affiliation.

Limits: text-only messages ≤1024 characters, ≤90 messages/client/second, action cooldown400ms. Names, Dome travel, mini-game entry and mini-game start/leave bypass the action cooldown but remain subject to the socket rate limit and simulation validation. Names follow the normalization and active-name collision policy described below. Invalid input closes1008; oversized/binary closes1009; inactive30seconds closes1001. Movement intent expires300ms after last input. Missing/malformed room route404, missing upgrade426, cross-origin browser upgrade403. A full room accepts the WebSocket then closes it with application code 4003; if no safe spawn is available it closes with 4002 without consuming a seat. Both conditions are retryable and distinguishable from a connection failure. Missing Origin is permitted for public nonbrowser clients; Origin checks are not authentication.

Upgrade and OTA requests carry a positive safe-integer request ID, echoed only to the requester with the server's success or rejection, including cooldown rejection. The UI must wait for the matching result and show timeouts without claiming success. OTA subscription state and billing use authoritative game time and delivery cash; remote purchases require an active subscription and a driven vehicle, while physical shops remain available without it.

Capacity: `MAX_PLAYERS`, default64, clamped2–128. One named Durable Object per room. Start timer with first connection; stop and reset simulation when empty. Each room's Mars fund is durable; players, cash, awards, vehicles and all other simulation state remain ephemeral. Do not replace with a global room object or client-authoritative coordinates.

## Mars mission

`shared/mars.ts` owns the $1,000,000,000 target, contribution validation and boarding eligibility. `World.mars.fund` is a nonnegative integer capped at the goal. A room accepts whole-dollar contributions only from a connected player's current session cash, deducts exactly the amount added, and caps the final contribution at the remaining goal. A DO storage write must succeed before cash is debited or success is acknowledged; Cloudflare storage input/output gates serialize writes and keep uncommitted results private. Per-room durable `mars-fund` is loaded before connections and retained across an empty room, restart and deployment. An explicit solo session has a separate local fund which disappears when that session ends. There is no contributor ledger, refund, identity binding or cross-room pool. Do not describe session cash or personal achievements as persistent.

Once funded, a player must be on the ground within 14 m of the shared Fremont boarding point, with both forward speed and lateral push magnitude below 1 m/s, to request boarding. The room awards `to-mars` to that player, broadcasts `launchAt` in simulation time, and returns a private result. Each player may board once per session; additional drivers joining an active 12-second launch do not restart it, while a later driver starts a new launch. The cinematic launch and victory presentation are client-side, and free roam continues afterwards. There is no playable Mars surface. Rates, request IDs, errors and timeouts are handled like other room actions. The fund is a long-term objective: current fares are small; higher-paying levels remain future work.

## Global chat and saved identities

Same-origin WebSocket `GET /api/chat` connects to a single global chat Durable Object, independent of game rooms and available during solo play while connected. The chat client sends `{type:"hello",name:string,code?:string}` first, then `{type:"message",text:string}`, `{type:"save"}`, or a `{type:"ping"}` heartbeat. The server answers `{type:"ready",playerId,name,messages}`, broadcasts `{type:"message",message:{id,playerId,name,text,time}}`, and privately returns `{type:"recovery",code}` for a successful save or `{type:"error",message}` when a request fails. An active saved identity is reported with `code:"identity_active"`; the client retries briefly before requiring action. Heartbeats receive no reply. It derives author IDs and names from the connection, not from message fields.

Names are NFKC-normalized, stripped of control and invisible direction/format characters, whitespace-collapsed and capped at 20 Unicode code points; an empty name becomes `Driver`. Active names are compared case-insensitively. A conflict adds the first available ` #2`, ` #3`, and so on, trimming the base if needed. Names are not reserved when disconnected. Game rooms use the same rule independently, so a room display name can differ from the global chat name. Room labels are self-asserted: they are not proof of a saved chat identity. The full server-issued UUID identifies a chat sender; a short on-screen prefix is for convenience and is not guaranteed unique.

An unsaved identity lasts for the connection. Saving generates a random 256-bit bearer secret; the code contains the public player UUID and secret. Only the SHA-256 verifier and saved name are stored in the chat object's durable storage. Saving again returns the same code, including after a successful recovery. A valid code restores the chat ID and saved name, subject to the active-name collision rule; a second simultaneous connection using the same identity is rejected. Anyone with a code can use that identity, so it is shown only to its owner and must never appear in URLs, broadcasts, history or logs. Losing the code loses access to the saved identity. This restores neither the room session nor cars, cash, achievements or game progress.

Chat messages are normalized and limited to 240 Unicode code points. The server accepts at most five messages per connection per 10 seconds and limits all chat requests. The in-memory hub permits at most 512 sockets, 24 simultaneous sockets and 60 connections per IP per minute, and 30 new saved identities per IP and 240 globally per hour; missing IPs share an unknown bucket. Connections inactive for three minutes are closed; the client sends a heartbeat every 25 seconds. These limits reset on object restart and are modest abuse brakes, not a capacity measurement or comprehensive moderation. At most 50 recent messages are kept in memory and supplied on join; a runtime restart clears them. Chat sockets currently keep the object awake while connected. Reconnect and failure states must remain visible; chat failure must not silently change room play. Render names and messages as text, never interpolated HTML.

## Client adapters

Rejoining the currently connected room preserves the seat; an actual disconnect ends that room session. UI can replace presentation but must preserve explicit connection/error/rejoin/solo paths. Never display stale multiplayer as live. Server stalls exceeding 6 seconds close the connection; initial connection times out after 10 seconds.

`Controls.read()` returns a complete input. On blur/hidden/menu, clear keys and pointers. Multitouch uses pointer IDs and capture; release on pointerup/cancel/lost capture. Avoid stuck acceleration. Every keyboard action needs a touch equivalent. UI dialogs must neutralize controls while preserving connection heartbeats.

`GameScene.render(world,playerId,dt,menu)` reads snapshots. `start(callback)` runs render frames. `cycleCamera()` cycles chase, cockpit and wide views. Cockpit follows the interpolated vehicle orientation (including pitch and roll); the player's exterior mesh is hidden locally. On foot the selected cockpit mode renders as chase and is not counted as cockpit. The projected destination sign and dashboard are local to the player; the physical objective ring and authoritative target remain shared. Camera, smoothing, labels and effects are local presentation; never modify physical coordinates to make visuals easier.

The client sets `Input.cockpit` only when cockpit is selected in a vehicle, gameplay is showing, and the tab is visible and focused; informational dialogs keep the selected view. The simulation ignores the claim on foot, tracks `Player.cockpit.active` and adds only delivery-active seconds to `activeSeconds`. A completed delivery increments `deliveries` only when all its active pickup and delivery time was reported in cockpit and at least one active cockpit step occurred for that order. Island, ferry and dome time pauses the order and does not affect its cockpit eligibility or order seconds, while global cockpit active time still accrues when the player is active in cockpit. `orderEligible` and `orderSeconds` are per-order simulation bookkeeping; a new order resets them. Stale inputs become neutral, so missing input during an active interval can disqualify the run. The room validates the flag's type, not the camera: these are self-reported session stats, not anti-cheat proof or a cash multiplier. Other UI/asset agents must preserve the claim, these distinctions, and visible labels.

## Fleet and autonomy contract

`shared/vehicles.ts` defines all model identities, dimensions, wheelbase, mass, steering, acceleration, braking, stock capabilities and provenance. Renderer consumes the same IDs and dimensions. Numbers marked estimates remain estimates; never silently promote them to manufacturer specifications. All handling is arcade tuning. The `roadster` has stock fictional Rocket capability. A Rocket pickup or purchased kit fills the tank and grants capability for the vehicle’s session. Kits belong to the vehicle, not its driver. Air jumps, free-air thruster hover, adhesion and tank performance are game fiction. Suction operates only on supported stunt surfaces.

Collision bodies are oriented rectangles using registry length/width; mass-weighted impulse response includes lateral velocity. Bicycle steering uses wheelbase and bounded steering angle, with quicker, tighter steering for manual drivers; Cybertruck/Zoox have enhanced low-speed steering and visually countersteering rear wheels. A grounded player car moving forward can drift by steering with brake; lateral momentum decays after release. Opposing throttle brakes to a stop before reversing and suppresses steering while changing direction. Powered airborne throttle damps opposing and sideways velocity to make aiming more responsive. This is not suspension/tire simulation. Terrain roof support remains shared.

Every model has one parked entity and one NPC traffic entity. `npc` is identity; `autopilot` is current control mode. NPCs have null `driver`, so on-foot players may take them; taking one disables autonomy and stops it. Human-occupied vehicles cannot be taken. Exiting/disconnecting releases an NPC back to street autonomy after a pause when it can attach to a route. Other empty vehicles remain parked unless explicitly enabled.

Autonomy uses deterministic right-lane loops, including both directions around the Bay, speed-dependent following gaps and a timed eastbound Bay Bridge approach signal. Traffic demand follows the accelerated day, while the signal and driving behavior use real simulation seconds. Engage only on ground within 9 m of a supported street route. A player's throttle/steer/brake/jump/flip/boost/hover/roll input immediately cancels AV mode. The server owns route state and motion; renderers never advance traffic. No real AV/vehicle API is used. Mode is available for every model as game fiction, including historical prototypes.

The expanded map can request a destination. `shared/navigation.ts` builds a directed graph from the existing traffic lanes, snaps to a nearby connected lane, and checks the player's unlocked regions along the route. A car already on a lane facing against its direction may continue in its current direction a short distance to an intersection before following directed streets. A destination requires a driven car near a supported ground route; water, disconnected island roads, locked areas, unreachable lanes and excessive detours are rejected. The server replies to the request ID, records the route and arrival state in the entity, and clears stale manual inputs on success. Manual input cancels the route. Obstructions can still hold a car; this is arcade navigation, not a real road planner. Choosing another destination replaces the route only on success.

Ambient demand targets up to 34 unattended autonomous highway cars alongside the 26 catalog NPCs and four police. Taken cars, off-route abandoned cars and cars reattached to city routes do not count toward that target; the optional ambient pool is capped at 68 in total, so replenishment can be delayed when its reserve is full. At most one ambient car is added or removed every five simulation seconds. Removal is beyond 1,600 m from every player and preserves occupied and stopped autonomous cars; hidden off-route abandoned cars and free-flowing city-route cars may be reclaimed when the pool blocks replenishment. Available hidden spawn sites and safe removal conditions also limit how quickly traffic follows demand.

Fleet selection accepts only Tesla IDs, requires supported speed≤1 m/s and is unavailable on the ferry or while attached to an arc. Other families remain drivable through street takeover. It releases the old vehicle, creates the requested player-owned model at a checked free spawn, and removes abandoned personal duplicates. IDs are strict allowlisted own registry properties (not `in` checks susceptible to prototype names). No client-supplied mass, coordinates or damage. Names bypass the action cooldown but remain rate-limited so startup selection isn't lost behind a name message. Protocol version constant is `PROTOCOL_VERSION` in shared/game.ts; incompatible clients explicitly reload.

Geometry/materials in the renderer are shared static caches. Removing an entity must NOT dispose shared geometry. Wheel assemblies are named `wheel_fl`, `wheel_fr`, `wheel_rl`, `wheel_rr`; yaw articulation uses shared steering state. `jets` contains independent rear, corner and side cold-gas vapor groups, instantiated for other models when upgraded. The simulation publishes a thruster bitmask so remote effects follow actual boost, jump, hover and roll events; firing patterns are arcade interpretations, not prototype control logic. Future models must pass catalog coverage, body collision, takeover, traffic-stability and multiplayer-selection tests.

## Island, ferry and activities

`shared/ferry.ts` owns the moving deck, phase, velocity, countdown and passenger offsets. Boarding is limited to a stationary docked ferry; departure requires each passenger to be fully on deck and stopped, with at most six vehicles. During a crossing attached passengers retain their deck-local positions and movement/exit controls are disabled. On arrival they can disembark, then remaining passengers are placed at a collision-checked local apron; a blocked apron holds the ferry. Reset uses a checked shore spawn. Do not present an animated or teleported object as the ferry's authoritative support.

Dock signs show the current status, countdown and vehicle occupancy from snapshots. The deck has painted walking lanes, pedestrian standing spots and gates that open visually at the corresponding dock; these markings and gates do not provide collision geometry. A nearby player hears a synthesized horn cue on a departure or arrival phase change. These are presentation cues; the simulation owns boarding, departure and passenger safety.

`shared/island-stunts.ts` defines a bank with tapered ground transitions and a separate parametric inverted cliff. Meshes and physics use the same vertices. Cliff attachment retains a local angle; Rocket-capable cars can adhere beyond vertical and ordinary cars detach or bounce. Airborne crossings use swept contacts and detachments preserve world velocity. Island and ferry support clear police pursuits and prevent detection; the current order timer pauses there while active-play time retains its normal rules. Previous tickets remain charged.

`shared/island-jumps.ts` defines two eastbound ramp tops, their normals and swept landing contacts. Cars enter from the level end, follow the shared slope and leave the lip with its upward velocity. The rendered wedges use the same coordinates; their bounded sides and high ends block grounded and airborne cars, including displacement from other cars.

`shared/oval-bank.ts` defines the speed oval's triangulated height and normals, shoulders and swept landing; banked support uses `supportId: 'island-oval'`, and the renderer follows those triangles. The race start/grid, short-course overlap and dock junction are flat and blend into the bank. The oval still uses the general arcade steering and traction model, not a realistic simulation of banking or a guarantee of high-speed grip.

`shared/parking-lots.ts` defines two playgrounds and 24 charging lots and `parking.ts` owns their bounded rounds. The client confirms entry, start and leave from snapshots and offers contextual controls and failure feedback.

## Shops and upgrades contract

`shared/upgrades.ts` owns fictional shop positions, range, upgrade IDs, prices and eligibility. Stop in a ground-level purple bay or have an active OTA subscription before sending `upgrade` with one of `rocket|economy|tank|motor|armor|service`. `buyUpgrade` independently validates driver, location, motion, cash, prerequisites and duplicates, and deducts the player’s session cash. The rocket kit grants any vehicle the flight controls and a full tank; economy reduces flight air use by 25%; tank upgrades double capacity, refill, and cost $30/$60/$120/$240 to reach levels 2/3/4/5; motor changes acceleration and road speed, armor halves impact damage, and service repairs/refills. Shop state belongs to each vehicle, not the player; an old personal car is removed on fleet selection and abandoned ambient traffic may be reclaimed. A room reset loses all upgrades and money. `rocket` pickups in the city, on the island and at parking playgrounds grant the same session-long kit without payment and respawn in 20 seconds. An equipped car consumes one only if it needs an air refill. Green repair and amber air diamonds each award $5 spendable session cash, without counting toward delivery earnings or progression. Shared simulation and room snapshots own all state; the UI and renderer must not apply purchases themselves. See [UPGRADES.md](UPGRADES.md).

## Delivery and police contract

`shared/delivery.ts` owns restaurant and fictional customer identities, artistic coordinates, order selection, active time and payment. Each player has an independent `delivery` state in snapshots. A grounded player within 9 m moving no faster than 4 m/s automatically picks up or delivers; no client message claims a payment. Pickup has its own active-time estimate based on distance from where the order began; reaching the restaurant early pays up to 40% of base fare immediately. The delivery estimate begins at pickup and counts active play. Active time includes control input plus a 3-second grace period, and a moving player AV. When idle, the pickup and delivery estimates and leaderboard clock pause. `lastReward` contains the most recent positive pickup or delivery award with a monotonically increasing per-player ID; a renderer may celebrate each new ID once, but cannot create payouts. Base fare depends on straight-line distance; completing early adds up to 75% of base, and lateness reduces fare to a floor of zero. See [DELIVERY.md](DELIVERY.md) for details and provenance.

Each session starts at level 0 with SF orders. `earned` accumulates positive pickup and delivery rewards, independent of the spendable `cash` wallet; tickets and purchases never subtract from it. Unlocks require both completed deliveries and earned: Oakland/Berkeley at 2/$50, Peninsula/East Bay at 5/$140, San Jose at 8/$350. `LEVELS`, `ACCESS_BOUNDARIES` and `requiredLevelAt(x,z)` in `shared/delivery.ts` define this contract. Each order selects only unlocked pickups and destinations; exploration jobs reach unlocked landmark regions, while other jobs may be local or eligible rooftops. The authoritative simulation blocks controlled cars or pedestrians from crossing into a locked region, including flight, collision displacement and vehicle takeover. NPCs are unaffected. `lastBlocked:{level,at}|null` records the required level and world time of the most recent blocked attempt; clients show requirements from `LEVELS`, and may not grant access. Rocket Dome and North Bay Playground remain open at all levels; the SF parking playground has a bounded level-zero access pocket. Progress is per player and per room session; saved chat identity does not restore it.

Four police NPCs patrol the shared world. A cop seeing a car between 3 m and 80 m above ground within 85 m starts pursuing the player; a parked car on a roof is not flying. Pursuit ends after 10 simulated seconds outside 110 m, when the player is ticketed, or when the player enters the protected island/ferry area or dome. When grounded and within 12 m, the car is slowed; within 7 m and below 4 m/s it receives a severity- and repeat-offense-based citation, followed by 20 seconds without re-detection. Police are not stealable. All detection, movement and cash changes are authoritative in the shared fixed-step simulation. Police pathfinding uses the compressed street grid; it is deliberately approximate.

`L` / Scores shows the current room's active players ranked by cash with active time and cash per active minute. Scores disappear on disconnect/reset; saved chat identities do not save scores and there is no global leaderboard. Neither clock nor leaderboard is a verified anti-cheat measure: room inputs are authoritative but anonymous clients can automate them.

## Rocket Dome soccer

`shared/dome.ts` defines the fictional China Basin waterfront arena and its north entrance; `shared/map.ts` supplies its land, plaza, street approach, capsule surface and support basis. `travelDome` moves one player and current vehicle or person between the arena and a checked Bay or island spawn, preserving health and air and returning them to the last supported Bay or island side (or the ferry destination). Cars can drive in from the street and out through the marked north gate without teleporting; vehicles can also enter through the sides once their upright bodies fit. Descents from above land on the exterior roof and overflights remain outside. Goal apertures contain cars. The arena has no police detection or citations, and pauses the current delivery order clock while leaving the active-play clock intact. Protocol v30 requires matching world geometry and soccer match state on both ends.

Gold arrows mark the air-charge zone: a Rocket-equipped vehicle attached to the dome at least 8 m above its floor and moving at least 5 m/s refills air at 24 percentage points per simulation second, capped at 100. It also works on the ceiling and while boosting, including with a Rocket kit from a pickup; stationary, airborne, floor-level and non-Rocket vehicles cannot charge. The shared simulation owns the change and clients display replicated air and eligibility.

`shared/soccer.ts` owns the one room-wide ball and colored scores. Any car can hit either goal; there are no player teams. Crossing a colored goal awards that color a point, freezes play for three simulation seconds, then returns the ball to center. The first color to five ends the match; after a six-second winner display, both scores, player match stats and the ball reset. The last driven car to physically strike the ball earns the goal for its driver and its class; an undriven car's strike clears the last touch. The player(s) present in the dome at match completion with the most credited goals win; if no eligible player has a credited goal, the match has no player winner. Leaving and returning before the finish retains the player’s match goals. Departing the room removes that driver's match stats and pending touch. Physics, scoring and timing are authoritative in online rooms; clients render snapshots and never send ball positions, goals or score claims. A new or empty room starts a new match.


## Citations, rooftops and achievements

`shared/police.ts` measures airborne duration and peak height in the authoritative simulation. When a pursuit ends in a citation, `delivery.lastTicket` records the amount, offense number, flight duration, height and the duration, height and repeat components. The total is deducted immediately, once, with a grace period. Presentation must make a citation conspicuous and require an accessible acknowledgement; clearing controls does not stop server heartbeats. A room continues running while a dialog is open. Session-local UI may not create tickets or alter their amount.

Rooftop customers in `shared/delivery.ts` select actual flat `BUILDINGS` from `shared/map.ts`; the objective marker uses their shared height. A rooftop dropoff requires the car to land and stop within 6 m of the roof center. After Oakland unlocks, an eligible non-exploration order after every third completed run starting with the first may select a rooftop only when the current vehicle has flight capability. Level-zero deliveries remain on the ground. Other vehicles receive ground orders. The player may change cars later, so the interface must identify rooftop orders and the fleet remains available.

`shared/achievements.ts` owns unlocked award IDs and flight tracking per player in snapshots. Bay Hopper requires leaving one mainland shore, crossing water, and arriving on the other without landing; a bridge, island, roof or stunt-loop contact ends the attempt, and recovery or vehicle switches cancel it. Flying above a bridge is allowed. Roof Rider requires a car on a building roof; Sky Delivery requires a completed rooftop delivery; Neighborhood Regular requires three deliveries. Dome awards require a real ball touch and the player to be in the dome at match completion. Rocket Rookie and Built to Fly require touches in Roadster · Rocket and a retrofit respectively; Roadster Champion and Garage Champion require a win and at least one goal credited in the corresponding class. No Rockets Needed requires a win with at least one stock-car goal and only stock-car touches. A tie counts as a win for each leader. Awards are ephemeral and do not affect cash. Clients can render progress and sequence notifications but cannot assert unlocks.
