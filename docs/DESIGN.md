# Tesland game design · simple fun first

## Product decisions

Approved: stylized compressed Bay Area; driving/stunts first; real Tesla Supercharger identities with fictional compressed-air refills; shared public rooms with more than16 capacity; desktop AND phone. Defer shooting and death/respawning but reserve a concrete design. The visual baseline is original low-poly geometry, not a requirement that later art must remain low-poly.

## Thirty-second loop

The game joins the public Bay room on arrival, or the named room in an invite link. A failed connection does not silently start local play. Start seated in a Roadster → accelerate → jump above a block → flip → land → find a charger → do it again with a friend. If the room is full, retry or name another room. No account or garage purchase is required; San Francisco and the Rocket Dome are immediately playable. Deliveries open more of the Bay during the session. Every participant starts with a usable vehicle and 100 air. Every researched family has parked and autonomous traffic examples. NPCs can be taken while nearby; human-occupied cars cannot be stolen. Fleet selection offers Teslas directly when stopped; other families must be taken from the street.

The simulation prioritizes arcade legibility over realism. Ground friction slows an unattended car. A rocket-equipped vehicle shares one fictional tank for boost, jumps, forward flight, hover and rolls. Level 1 has twice the former capacity: a full jump uses 6%, a flip 4.5%, boost 7% per second, hover 9% per second and forward thrust 2% per second. Tank upgrades at fictional shops double capacity at each level through Level 5. Air percentages are relative to the installed tank. W/S provide forward/reverse thrust in the air; hover holds altitude; powered forward or reverse flight corrects sideways and opposing momentum for easier aiming. Jump uses whatever air remains for a proportional launch, and holding the jump control sustains hover after a short delay. A tank upgrade changes capacity, not jump strength. Air jumps restore upward velocity without stacking a second full impulse. Air jumps and free-air hover are arcade fiction. Fictional underbody fans adhere rocket-equipped vehicles to the purpose-built loop without consuming compressed air and cannot hold it in free air. A depleted tank never prevents ordinary driving or falling. Station refills take about 3.6 seconds from empty, only when grounded and stopped within 9 m of a station or in a marked charger bay. Supply diamonds provide instant full air or repair plus $5, with a 20 second respawn; the cash is committed to the saved wallet. Condition is clamped to 5 rather than introducing death before its design exists.

## Delivery loop

Follow the gold marker to a notable restaurant, stop to collect an order, then deliver it to a fictional address. A fast pickup pays an immediate bonus. A separate estimate starts after pickup; beating it adds a delivery bonus and arriving late reduces the fare, down to a minimum of half the order's base fare. The HUD shows current cash and active play time, and Scores compares the current room. Unattended time pauses after a short grace period. Driving and stunts remain available without completing orders.

Players start at level zero. Current cash opens Oakland and Berkeley, then the Peninsula and East Bay, then San Jose and later career tiers; a purchase or fine can lower the level. The overview expands and destinations include unlocked landmarks as the world opens. The shared simulation applies the same boundaries to ground and flying drivers, and a player already inside a relocked area can leave. The saved wallet retains progress, achievements and owned vehicles across sessions. Scores show current and recently observed mini-game results for the drive, and Awards shows progress and save status. The HUD shows the next milestone and explains a blocked crossing. This follows the clear, pinned milestone pattern of [Forza Horizon's adventure progression](https://forza.net/news/forza-horizon-5-first-drive), adapted to this sandbox.

Rocket flight can shorten a run but nearby police may spot it, pursue and slow the car before issuing a severity- and repeat-offense-based citation. Orders for rocket-equipped vehicles sometimes target a physical rooftop, and achievements reward crossing the Bay without landing, rooftop play and deliveries. Escaping their range or accepting a ticket ends a chase. All cash, timing and police behavior run in the shared simulation. Restaurants have no game affiliation, the geography and customers are fictionalized, and cash is not real money. See [DELIVERY.md](DELIVERY.md).

The cooperative $1 billion Mars goal advances automatically from eligible lifetime earnings across all drivers. Spending does not reduce that total. Each room also retains its separate, persistent voluntary fund and its existing contributions. Once either goal is reached, a player can reach Fremont at level 2 and stop in the boarding ring to launch and earn an achievement. Free roam remains available afterwards. Contributor recognition and season retirement remain future work. See [CONTRACTS.md](CONTRACTS.md#mars-mission).

The Rocket Dome sits at the compressed China Basin ballpark site, with a street-facing drive-through gate, a monumental translucent roof, orange-and-black stands and original baseball-inspired statues. The [Giants' ballpark guide](https://www.mlb.com/giants/ballpark/services-and-locations) informs the waterfront plaza and entrance, but the venue and art are fictional.

In the Rocket Dome, either colored goal counts; a color reaching five ends the match, and the driver in the dome with the most last-touch goals wins, with ties shared. Touching the ball and finishing a match earns an achievement for Roadster · Rocket or a non-factory rocket retrofit. Wins with a credited goal unlock their corresponding champion awards; a stock-only touch run can earn No Rockets Needed. Rocket pickups install a session-long retrofit and qualify for the retrofit awards. Players must still be in the dome at the finish. Like [Rocket League's play, win and ball-hit challenges](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/get-the-havoc-car-body-as-rivals-and-rockets-kicks-off-april-17/), the awards distinguish participation from performance without adding a separate currency.

The research tour gives drivers four useful detours between deliveries. Blue rings at UCSF, Berkeley, Stanford and SFO award cash; Berkeley, Stanford and SFO also unlock workshop discounts stored with the saved driver. A delivery to SFO adds an airport achievement. Awards lists the destinations and their rewards. The small landmark challenges follow the discover-and-reward pattern of [Forza Horizon's Pathfinder accolades](https://forza.net/news/forza-horizon-5-explore-the-horizon). The landmarks and research activities are game interpretations and have no university or airport affiliation. Rules and amounts are in [CONTRACTS.md](CONTRACTS.md#citations-rooftops-and-achievements).

## Rocket Dome wall charge

Gold arrows on the curved walls mark an air refill zone. A Rocket-equipped car moving above the gold line (8 m), including on the ceiling, recharges air. The stadium's lights, seating and wall play draw on Psyonix's descriptions of [Champions Field](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/rocket-league-anniversary-update-july-5), [walls as attacking surfaces](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/new-rocket-labs-arena---pillars), and [boost placement shaping play](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/head-to-the-boostfield-mall-in-rocket-league-season-20). Continuous wall recharge is this game's own mechanic; the art is original procedural geometry.

## Current content

A compressed Bay circuit connects San Francisco, the Peninsula, San Jose, Fremont, Oakland and the Bay Bridge; the Golden Gate remains a Marin branch. Highway traces start from OpenStreetMap and are transformed and joined with fictional roads; streets, shorelines, landmarks and districts are authored approximations, not navigation data. The circuit measures about 19.9 game km. The Oakland launch ramp and stunt loop are shared physical surfaces; water is below the roads and unsupported water landings recover the car to a safe street. See [MAP.md](MAP.md). Twenty Tesla-listed station identities and four fictional air stations in Berkeley, Mill Valley and Corte Madera use artistic coordinates; air refilling and flight are fictional. See [station sources](STATIONS.md).

Controls: steering plus forward/reverse; separate brake. While a player car is moving forward, braking into a turn starts an arcade slide; release to regain grip. Opposing throttle first brakes to a stop without steering toward the old direction, then begins moving and steering in the requested direction. This arcade assist draws on automatic direction-changing conventions described in [Arma Reforger’s vehicle simulation](https://community.bistudio.com/wiki/Arma_Reforger%3AVehicle%3A_Wheeled_Simulation) and [Rumpus Engine’s vehicle manual](https://www.rumpusengine.com/breach-help.html). Person movement uses the same turn-and-move arcade controls. Exit requires grounded speed≤5m/s; entry selects closest empty car within7m. Unstuck recovery repositions the current entity while preserving condition/air and is not death/respawn.

## Visual direction

Warm coastal daylight, desaturated sea/teal city, cream type, amber player/actions, mint charger markers. Car silhouettes and roadway contrast matter more than texture detail. Vehicle airborne state, remaining air, repair state, nearest station distance and visible other players must remain readable. Keep minimal instruments; do not turn the screen into a dashboard of implementation metrics.

Alternatives can change style, HUD organization and models. Preserve the same gameplay entry points, touch equivalents and state transitions. See ASSETS.md for geometry/attachment budgets. Brand name is Tesland (formerly Poly AV and Bay Drive); Poly Ave remains an in-world street. The setting is an independently fictional and satirical 2050 Bay Area. Tesla Roadster is an in-game vehicle identity, with no claim of official affiliation or real flight capability.

## Multiplayer scale

Target is a busy small playground, not a single giant universe. The default admission limit is 64 participants per room, but playable performance at that occupancy has not been established. Multiple independent rooms use separate Durable Objects. A platform connection limit is not a simulation capacity promise. The implementation sends recipient-filtered 10 Hz snapshots and uses a spatial index for entity collisions. Measure CPU, memory, outbound bytes, snapshot gaps, input-to-observed latency and mobile rendering at the intended occupancy; see the [overflow plan](ROOM_OVERFLOW_PLAN.md) for a future way to place additional public players.

Snapshots always retain players and occupied cars, plus nearby ambient entities; the room remains authoritative for the entire world. Building and entity collisions use spatial indexes. The client predicts local motion and reconciles against tick-stamped authoritative snapshots. Driver wallets persist across rooms; the rest of the physical world remains room-local. Compact delta snapshots are a possible future optimization.

## Deferred combat and respawning — design only

**First combat mode:** opt-in bumper/paint-tag arena, separate from friendly free-roam. No weapons active in baseline. Add a room ruleset chosen before joining; do not let one client change a live room's consent model. Primary fire gets both mouse/keyboard and a touch button.

**Authority:** extend client protocol with `fire` intent containing a bounded sequence and aim direction, never target ID/damage/hit claims. Server derives muzzle from entity pose, enforces cooldown/ammunition, simulates projectile lifetime, resolves collision against shared geometry, and broadcasts authoritative shot/hit events with stable IDs. Version the protocol. Visual projectiles/muzzle flashes consume events; they cannot deal damage. A basic bounded projectile simulation is simpler than rewind lag compensation for a first arcade mode.

**Damage:** shared entity condition remains the resource. Add explicit active/disabled state and per-mode damage policy. Decide friendly fire in the room ruleset. Spawn/charger safe zones prevent camping; disabled players retain menu/camera access. No paid advantage.

**Respawn:** after a short visible countdown, server chooses an unoccupied safe spawn, resets vehicle resources, grants brief server-timed invulnerability, and emits a respawn event. Clients never teleport/reset resources on their own. Tests must cover duplicate fire, cooldown, forged hits, projectile expiry, disconnect mid-shot, spawn occupancy and invulnerability expiry. Keep existing unstuck recovery separate and prevent it from evading active combat.

**Persistence:** none required for this feature. Add cosmetic/session score first; accounts and permanent progression need a separate product decision.

## Next improvements in order

1. Human playtest on a real iPhone and Android: steering, jump/flip/hover reachability, heat and frame rate.
2. Vehicle feel: tune camera, air time, collisions and refill loop from playtests.
3. Improve district art while preserving registry model pivots and collision volumes.
4. Playful destinations: shared stunt rings, short time trials, small ramps using authoritative terrain geometry.
5. Extend persistent progression in playable layers; treat opt-in combat and respawning as separate future work.

Keep the delivery and driving loops satisfying as the persistent economy grows; add matchmaking, moderation and broad asset infrastructure only when needed.

## Tesland fleet layer

26 researched vehicle/platform families: 11 Tesla, 9 Waymo, 4 Cruise, 2 Zoox. Catalog scope includes historical and announced designs; trims and unnamed internal chassis are not extra models. See FLEET_RESEARCH.md for every source and uncertainty.

The new loop is drive → see an interesting AV → exit → take it → feel its weight/turning radius → enable AV and watch it cruise → manually take over. Fleet selection makes Teslas easy to find; the other families remain street takeovers. All model families can drive themselves in the game regardless of real-world autonomy capability. Wheel-less robotaxis receive fictional manual controls.

Vehicle collections reward trying the roster. Drive each Tesla and Vault model for 100 m in a ride, and take distinct NPC or seeded parked fleet models from the street; the Awards collection shows what remains. This combines a named collection goal with actual driving, following Forza Horizon 5's [car-collection accolades](https://forza.net/news/forza-horizon-5-summer-party) and [distance-based driving challenges](https://forza.net/news/forza-horizon-5-high-performance). The thresholds and cash rewards are Tesland's own rules.

Traffic follows deterministic city and Bay loops with speed-dependent following gaps. Double-tapping Shift or using the delivery button requests a route toward the current order. Street Pilot can try a checked off-road join and bounded recovery manoeuvres when blocked. The fictional Ludicrous Leap and Maximum Plaid shop upgrades add opportunistic traffic hops and flight using suitable thermals or air streams. They need rocket equipment and fall back to the road when conditions are unsuitable. Rooftop deliveries still require manual flight. A bridge-approach meter produces a red-light queue and releases it on green; a periodic unattended highway car can slow to a stop with flashing hazards and clear its queue afterward. A 48-minute day changes lighting and gradually changes the bounded ambient traffic population; physics and signal timing remain in real simulation seconds. This is a simple arcade planner: water, disconnected island roads and locked areas remain unavailable, and some obstructions cannot be recovered. Ordinary manual input cancels AV immediately. Only the fictional next-generation Roadster starts with air thrusters. Any vehicle can acquire session-long flight through a Rocket pickup or paid workshop kit; upgrades and their tradeoffs are described in [UPGRADES.md](UPGRADES.md).

The affectionate SF wordplay is “See other vehicles,” “Many rides. Zero commitment,” and “Open relationships. Open roads.” Keep jokes about car choice and low polygon counts rather than mocking people's relationships.

## Road-first HUD · 2026-10-17

Selected hybrid: the road-first desktop concept and pocket-console phone concept. Acceptance traits: (1) the road remains the dominant view, with mission, map, and instruments at the edges; (2) secondary actions and the keyboard guide open on demand; (3) touch controls form two tactile thumb zones, with a separate reachable flight row. The large Drive button remains available in the air, with a separate one-tap Flip button. The running game retains its existing low-poly world, real mission text, telemetry and all actions rather than the generated mock's illustrative content.

The free [Mobbin explorer](https://mobbin.com/) informed disclosure and grouped-control patterns; deeper app examples required sign-in. [EA SPORTS WRC's PC controls](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-wrc/wrc-24/game-objects/wrc-controls-hub/game-object/driving-controls-pc-keyboard) use Space for handbrake, a useful precedent for this game's Space brake/drift. Generated references: [desktop concept](screenshots/road-first-hud/desktop-concept.png), [phone concept](screenshots/road-first-hud/phone-concept.png). The phone adaptation keeps roll, combined jump/hover, reverse and brake, which were not all represented in the mock; landscape moves the flight row to the center so both thumb zones remain clear.
